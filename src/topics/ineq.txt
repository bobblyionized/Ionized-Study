<script type="text/plain" id="L-ineq-solve">
<h2>Solving Inequalities</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Rules</h4><ul>
<li>Multiplying or dividing by a <b>negative</b> number flips the inequality.</li>
<li><b>Never multiply by a variable expression</b> whose sign you do not know. Move everything to one side and use a sign chart.</li>
<li>Quadratic $(x-a)(x-b)\lt 0$ with $a\lt b$: the solution is $a\lt x\lt b$ (between the roots). For $>0$, it is outside the roots.</li>
<li>$|x-a|\lt b$ means $a-b\lt x\lt a+b$; $|x-a|>b$ means $x\lt a-b$ or $x>a+b$.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Sign charts</h3>
<p>Mark every zero of the numerator and denominator on a number line. Test one point per interval; the sign flips at each simple zero. Denominator zeros are never included.</p>
<h3>Counting integer solutions</h3>
<p>Find the interval, then count carefully: the integers in $a\lt x\lt b$ (with $a,b$ integers) number $b-a-1$.</p>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example.</b> How many integers $n$ satisfy $(n^2-9)(n^2-49)\lt 0$?
<details><summary>Solution</summary>We need $9\lt n^2\lt 49$, so $|n|\in\{4,5,6\}$, giving $6$ integers.</details></div>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-ineq-amgm">
<h2>AM–GM</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>The inequality</h4>
For nonnegative reals: $\dfrac{a_1+\dots+a_n}{n}\ge\sqrt[n]{a_1\cdots a_n}$, with equality exactly when all $a_i$ are equal.</div>
<h3>Two uses</h3>
<ul><li><b>Fixed product, minimize the sum:</b> $x+\frac{16}{x}\ge2\sqrt{16}=8$, with equality at $x=4$. In general $ax+\frac bx\ge2\sqrt{ab}$.</li>
<li><b>Fixed sum, maximize the product:</b> if $x+2y=12$, then $x\cdot2y\le\left(\frac{12}2\right)^2=36$, so $xy\le18$.</li></ul>
<h3>Splitting terms</h3>
<p>To minimize $x^2+\frac2x$, split it as $x^2+\frac1x+\frac1x\ge3\sqrt[3]{1}=3$, with equality at $x=1$. Choose the split so the product is constant.</p>
<h3>Standard results</h3>
<ul><li>$(a+b)\left(\frac1a+\frac1b\right)\ge4$</li>
<li>$abc=k^3$ implies $a+b+c\ge3k$</li>
<li>$x+\frac1x\ge2$ for $x>0$</li></ul>
<div class="warn"><h4>Always check equality</h4>The bound is the answer only if equality can actually occur under the problem's constraints.</div>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-ineq-cs">
<h2>Cauchy–Schwarz &amp; QM–AM</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Cauchy–Schwarz</h4>
$(a_1b_1+\dots+a_nb_n)^2\le(a_1^2+\dots+a_n^2)(b_1^2+\dots+b_n^2)$, with equality when the vectors are proportional.</div>
<h3>Standard applications</h3>
<ul><li>Max of $ax+by$ on $x^2+y^2=r^2$: $r\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$. (Also: $a\sin\theta+b\cos\theta\le\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$.)</li>
<li>Min of $x^2+y^2$ given $ax+by=c$: $\dfrac{c^2}{a^2+b^2}$, the squared distance from the origin to the line.</li>
<li>Min of $\sum x_i^2$ given $\sum x_i=S$ ($n$ variables): $\frac{S^2}{n}$, at equal values.</li>
<li><b>Titu's lemma:</b> $\dfrac{a^2}{x}+\dfrac{b^2}{y}\ge\dfrac{(a+b)^2}{x+y}$. For example $\frac1a+\frac4b\ge\frac{(1+2)^2}{a+b}$.</li>
<li><b>QM–AM:</b> $\sqrt{\frac{a^2+b^2+c^2}{3}}\ge\frac{a+b+c}{3}$.</li></ul>
<div class="tip"><h4>Geometry view</h4>Minimizing $(x-p)^2+(y-q)^2$ on a line is a squared point-to-line distance: $\frac{(ap+bq-c)^2}{a^2+b^2}$.</div>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-ineq-abs">
<h2>Absolute Value &amp; Triangle Inequality</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Facts</h4><ul>
<li>$|a+b|\le|a|+|b|$ and $|a-b|\ge\big||a|-|b|\big|$.</li>
<li>$\sum|x-a_i|$ is minimized when $x$ is a <b>median</b> of the $a_i$.</li>
<li>Triangle with sides $a,b,c$: $|a-b|\lt c\lt a+b$ (strict, or the triangle is degenerate).</li>
<li>The region $|x|+|y|\le k$ is a square (diamond) with diagonals $2k$, so its area is $2k^2$.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Counting triangles</h3>
<p>For integer sides with a fixed perimeter $P$, list $a\le b\le c$ with $c\lt \frac P2$ (from $c\lt a+b=P-c$). Count systematically by the largest side.</p>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example.</b> Minimize $|x-1|+|x-2|+\dots+|x-9|$.
<details><summary>Solution</summary>At the median $x=5$: $4+3+2+1+0+1+2+3+4=20$.</details></div>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-ineq-opt">
<h2>Maximum &amp; Minimum Problems</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Strategy menu</h4><ul>
<li><b>Complete the square</b> in each variable: $x^2+4y^2-4x+8y+11=(x-2)^2+4(y+1)^2+3\ge3$.</li>
<li><b>Vertex of a parabola</b> for one-variable quadratics.</li>
<li><b>AM–GM or Cauchy</b> when a product or a sum of squares is constrained.</li>
<li><b>Integers:</b> check values near the real optimum. With $x+y=15$ (integers), $xy$ is largest at $7\cdot8$.</li>
<li><b>Max product of positive integers with sum $n$:</b> use as many 3s as possible, and use 2s (never 1s) for the remainder. For $n=10$: $3\cdot3\cdot4=36$.</li>
<li>Check the <b>boundary</b> of a restricted domain as well.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Geometric optimizations</h3>
<ul><li>Fixed perimeter: a square (or circle) maximizes area.</li>
<li>A rectangle inscribed in a semicircle of radius $1$: area $2\cos\theta\sin\theta=\sin2\theta\le1$.</li></ul>
</script>

A: 56

W: 54

W: 57
S: With $y=15-x$, $xy=x(15-x)$, a downward parabola peaking at $x=7.5$.
$x$ must be an integer, so take the closest: $x=7$ or $8$. Either way $xy=7\cdot8=56$.

Q: What is the smallest value of $(x+1)^2+(x-3)^2$?
L: 1
A: 8
W: 16
W: 4
W: 10
W: 0
S: Expand: $(x+1)^2+(x-3)^2=2x^2-4x+10=2(x-1)^2+8$.
Minimum $8$ at $x=1$, the midpoint of $-1$ and $3$ (each term is then $4$).

Q: Real numbers satisfy $x^2+y^2=18$. What is the largest possible value of $xy$?
L: 1
A: 9
W: 18
W: 6
W: 3\sqrt{2}
W: 36
S: $(x-y)^2\ge0$ gives $x^2+y^2\ge2xy$, so $2xy\le18$ and $xy\le9$.
Equality when $x=y$: $2x^2=18$, $x=y=3$.
