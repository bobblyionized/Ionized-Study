<h2>3D Geometry</h2>
<p>AMC 10 solid geometry is mostly computing volumes, surface areas, and distances, plus a few counting problems about cubes. The trick is usually to find the right 2D cross-section or the right right triangle.</p>

<h3>1. Volume</h3>
<div class="box"><h4>Formulas</h4><ul>
<li>Prism or cylinder: $V=(\text{base area})\times h$. Cylinder: $\pi r^2h$.</li>
<li>Pyramid or cone: $V=\frac13(\text{base area})\times h$. Cone: $\frac13\pi r^2h$.</li>
<li>Sphere: $V=\frac43\pi r^3$, surface area $4\pi r^2$.</li></ul></div>
<p><b>Why the $\frac13$.</b> A cube can be cut into $3$ congruent square pyramids, each with a face of the cube as its base and the opposite corner as its apex. So each has volume $\frac13s^3=\frac13(s^2)(s)$. <b>Cavalieri's principle</b> (solids with equal cross-section areas at every height have equal volume) extends this to every pyramid and cone with the same base area and height.</p>
<p><b>Sphere.</b> Archimedes showed that a sphere has $\frac23$ the volume of the cylinder around it: $\frac23\cdot\pi r^2\cdot2r=\frac43\pi r^3$. (Cavalieri: a hemisphere's cross-section at height $y$ has area $\pi(r^2-y^2)$, the same as a cylinder of radius $r$ with a cone removed, so hemisphere $=\pi r^3-\frac13\pi r^3=\frac23\pi r^3$.)</p>

<h3>2. Surface area</h3>
<ul><li>Box $a\times b\times c$: $2(ab+bc+ca)$.</li>
<li>Cylinder: $2\pi r^2+2\pi rh$ (unroll the side into a rectangle $2\pi r$ by $h$).</li>
<li>Cone: lateral area $\pi r\ell$, where $\ell=\sqrt{r^2+h^2}$ is the slant height. <b>Proof:</b> unrolled, the side is a sector of radius $\ell$ whose arc is the base circumference $2\pi r$; a sector's area is $\frac12\cdot$radius$\cdot$arc $=\frac12\ell\cdot2\pi r$.</li>
<li>Pyramid: base plus triangles; each triangle's height is the slant height, not the pyramid's height.</li></ul>

<h3>3. Distances</h3>
<ul><li><b>Space diagonal</b> of an $a\times b\times c$ box: $\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}$. Apply Pythagoras twice: the face diagonal is $\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$, and it is perpendicular to the edge $c$.</li>
<li>Cube of side $s$: face diagonal $s\sqrt2$, space diagonal $s\sqrt3$.</li>
<li><b>Shortest path along the surface:</b> unfold (make a net) so the path lies flat, then use a straight line. From one corner of a unit cube to the opposite corner along the surface: unfold two faces into a $1\times2$ rectangle, giving $\sqrt5$.</li></ul>

<h3>4. Scaling</h3>
<p>Scaling a solid by $k$ multiplies lengths by $k$, areas by $k^2$, and volumes by $k^3$. Increasing every edge of a cube by $50\%$ multiplies the volume by $1.5^3=3.375$.</p>

<h3>5. Counting on solids</h3>
<ul><li><b>Euler's formula:</b> for a convex polyhedron, $V-E+F=2$. (Cube: $8-12+6=2$.)</li>
<li><b>Painted cube:</b> an $n\times n\times n$ cube painted outside and cut into unit cubes has $8$ cubes with $3$ painted faces (corners), $12(n-2)$ with $2$ (edges), $6(n-2)^2$ with $1$ (faces), and $(n-2)^3$ with none.</li></ul>

<h3>6. Regular tetrahedron and other facts</h3>
<ul><li>Regular tetrahedron with edge $s$: volume $\frac{s^3}{6\sqrt2}$. It fits inside a cube of side $\frac s{\sqrt2}$ using alternate corners; remove four corner pyramids, each $\frac16$ of the cube, leaving $\frac13$ of the cube.</li>
<li><b>Water displacement:</b> an object fully submerged raises the water level by (object volume) $\div$ (container base area).</li></ul>

Q: What is the length of the space diagonal of a $3\times4\times12$ box?
L: 1
A: 13
W: 19
W: 12
W: 5
W: \sqrt{153}
S: The diagonal of the $3\times4$ face is $5$. That diagonal and the edge $12$ are perpendicular, so the space diagonal is $\sqrt{5^2+12^2}=13$.
(Formula: $\sqrt{9+16+144}=\sqrt{169}=13$.)

Q: A sphere has radius $3$ and a cylinder has radius $3$ and height $4$. What is the ratio of the sphere's volume to the cylinder's volume?
L: 1
A: 1
W: \frac34
W: \frac43
W: \frac12
W: 2
S: Sphere: $\frac43\pi\cdot27=36\pi$. Cylinder: $\pi\cdot9\cdot4=36\pi$.
The ratio is $1$: they have equal volume.

Q: A cone has base radius $6$ and height $8$. What is its lateral (curved) surface area?
L: 1
A: 60\pi
W: 48\pi
W: 96\pi
W: 36\pi
W: 100\pi
S: The slant height is $\ell=\sqrt{6^2+8^2}=10$.
Lateral area $=\pi r\ell=\pi\cdot6\cdot10=60\pi$. (Its volume would be $\frac13\pi\cdot36\cdot8=96\pi$.)

Q: What is the total surface area of a $2\times3\times4$ rectangular box?
L: 1
A: 52
W: 24
W: 26
W: 48
W: 56
S: Three pairs of faces: $2(2\cdot3+3\cdot4+2\cdot4)=2(6+12+8)=52$.

Q: A $4\times4\times4$ cube is painted on the outside and cut into $64$ unit cubes. How many unit cubes have paint on exactly two faces?
L: 1
A: 24
W: 8
W: 12
W: 32
W: 36
S: Cubes with exactly two painted faces lie along the edges but not at the corners. Each of the $12$ edges has $4-2=2$ such cubes.
$12\cdot2=24$.

Q: What is the volume of a regular tetrahedron with edge length $6$?
L: 3
A: 18\sqrt2
W: 36\sqrt2
W: 9\sqrt2
W: 27\sqrt3
W: 72
S: Use $V=\frac{s^3}{6\sqrt2}=\frac{216}{6\sqrt2}=\frac{36}{\sqrt2}=18\sqrt2$.
(Derivation: the tetrahedron sits in a cube of side $\frac6{\sqrt2}=3\sqrt2$, whose volume is $54\sqrt2$; removing four corner pyramids, each $\frac16$ of the cube, leaves $\frac13$ of it, $18\sqrt2$.)

Q: A square pyramid has base edge $6$ and height $4$. What is its total surface area?
L: 2
A: 96
W: 84
W: 60
W: 36+24\sqrt2
W: 144
S: The slant height (height of each triangular face) runs from the apex to the midpoint of a base edge: $\sqrt{4^2+3^2}=5$.
Four triangles: $4\cdot\frac12\cdot6\cdot5=60$. Base: $36$. Total: $96$.

Q: Each edge of a cube is increased by $50\%$. By what percent does the volume increase?
L: 1
A: 237.5\%
W: 50\%
W: 150\%
W: 125\%
W: 337.5\%
S: Volume scales by the cube of the length factor: $1.5^3=3.375$.
That is an increase of $3.375-1=2.375$, or $237.5\%$.
---
Q: An ant walks along the surface of a cube with edge $1$ from one corner to the opposite corner. What is the shortest possible distance?
L: 2
A: \sqrt5
W: \sqrt3
W: 2
W: 3
W: 1+\sqrt2
S: Unfold the two faces the ant crosses into a flat $1\times2$ rectangle; the opposite corners become opposite corners of the rectangle.
The straight-line distance is $\sqrt{1^2+2^2}=\sqrt5\approx2.24$. (The space diagonal $\sqrt3$ goes through the inside, which isn't allowed.)

Q: A cylindrical glass with radius $4$ holds water. A solid ball of radius $3$ is dropped in and is completely submerged. By how much does the water level rise?
L: 2
A: \frac94
W: 3
W: \frac{27}{16}
W: \frac{9}{16}
W: 2
S: The water rises by the ball's volume divided by the glass's base area.
Ball: $\frac43\pi\cdot27=36\pi$. Base: $16\pi$. Rise: $\frac{36\pi}{16\pi}=\frac94$.
---
Q: A convex polyhedron has $12$ vertices and $8$ faces. How many edges does it have?
L: 1
A: 18
W: 20
W: 16
W: 22
W: 24
S: Euler's formula: $V-E+F=2$, so $12-E+8=2$ and $E=18$.
(A hexagonal prism is an example.)
---
Q: A plane cuts a unit cube through three vertices that are each adjacent to one corner of the cube. What is the area of the triangular cross-section?
L: 3
A: \frac{\sqrt3}2
W: \frac12
W: \frac{\sqrt3}{4}
W: \frac{\sqrt2}2
W: 1
S: Each side of the triangle is a face diagonal of the cube, length $\sqrt2$, so the triangle is equilateral.
Area $=\frac{\sqrt3}4(\sqrt2)^2=\frac{\sqrt3}2$.
