<script type="text/plain" id="L-binom-coef">
<h2>Binomial Coefficients &amp; Pascal's Triangle</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Facts</h4><ul>
<li>$\binom nk=\frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}=\binom n{n-k}$ (symmetry). So $\binom na=\binom nb$ with $a\ne b$ means $n=a+b$.</li>
<li><b>Pascal:</b> $\binom nk=\binom{n-1}{k-1}+\binom{n-1}{k}$, so neighbors in a row add to the entry below.</li>
<li>Row $n$ sums to $2^n$. The even-position entries and the odd-position entries each sum to $2^{n-1}$.</li>
<li>The largest entry in row $n$ is in the middle: $\binom{n}{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$.</li>
<li><b>Parity (Lucas):</b> the number of odd entries in row $n$ is $2^{s}$, where $s$ is the number of 1s in $n$'s binary expansion.</li>
</ul></div>
<p>Small values to know: $\binom{n}{2}=\frac{n(n-1)}2$, $\binom{10}{3}=120$, $\binom{8}{4}=70$, $\binom{12}6=924$.</p>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-binom-exp">
<h2>Binomial Expansion</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Binomial theorem</h4>
$(x+y)^n=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk x^{n-k}y^k$. The general term is $\binom nk x^{n-k}y^k$.</div>
<h3>Finding one coefficient</h3>
<ul><li>In $(ax+b)^n$ the coefficient of $x^m$ is $\binom nm a^mb^{n-m}$. Keep track of negative signs.</li>
<li><b>Constant term</b> of $\left(x^p+\frac{c}{x^q}\right)^n$: write the general term's exponent as $p(n-k)-qk$ and set it to $0$.</li>
<li>$(1+x)^a(1+x)^b=(1+x)^{a+b}$.</li>
<li>Sum of all coefficients: plug in $x=1$.</li>
<li>Trinomials such as $(1+x+x^2)^n$: count the ways to pick exponents that add up to the target.</li></ul>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-binom-id">
<h2>Identities &amp; Multinomials</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Identities</h4><ul>
<li>$\sum_k\binom nk=2^n$ and $\sum_k(-1)^k\binom nk=0$ (for $n\ge1$).</li>
<li>$\sum_k k\binom nk=n2^{n-1}$ (choose a committee and then its leader).</li>
<li><b>Vandermonde:</b> $\sum_k\binom mk\binom n{r-k}=\binom{m+n}{r}$. In particular $\sum_k\binom nk^2=\binom{2n}{n}$.</li>
<li>Hockey stick: $\sum_{k=r}^n\binom kr=\binom{n+1}{r+1}$.</li>
<li>$\sum_k\frac{1}{k+1}\binom nk=\frac{2^{n+1}-1}{n+1}$ (integrate $(1+x)^n$).</li>
</ul></div>
<div class="box"><h4>Multinomial</h4>
The coefficient of $a^ib^jc^k$ in $(a+b+c)^n$ (with $i+j+k=n$) is $\dfrac{n!}{i!\,j!\,k!}$.</div>
<p>Subsets with an even number of elements: $2^{n-1}$ (exactly half of all subsets, for $n\ge1$).</p>
</script>

Q: What is $\dbinom{10}{3}$?
L: 1
A: 120
W: 720
W: 30
W: 210
W: 1000
S: $\binom{10}{3}$ counts the ways to choose $3$ objects from $10$ when order doesn't matter.
Formula: $\binom nk=\dfrac{n(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1)}{k!}$, with $k$ factors on top.
$\dbinom{10}{3}=\dfrac{10\cdot9\cdot8}{3\cdot2\cdot1}=\dfrac{720}{6}=120$.
($720$ is the number of <i>ordered</i> selections; dividing by $3!=6$ removes the orderings of each group.)

Q: If $\dbinom n2=45$, what is $n$?
L: 1
A: 10
W: 9
W: 15
W: 45
W: 90
S: Write the binomial coefficient as a formula: $\dbinom n2=\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}$.
Set it equal to $45$: $\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}=45$, so $n(n-1)=90$.
$90=10\cdot9$, two consecutive integers, so $n=10$.
Check: $\binom{10}{2}=\frac{10\cdot9}{2}=45$. ✓ (This is also the number of handshakes among $10$ people.)

If $\binom n4=\binom n6$ with $4\ne6$, the only way is $6=n-4$, i.e. $n=4+6=10$.
Check: $\binom{10}{4}=\binom{10}{6}=210$. ✓

A: 256

W: 255
S: Row $n$ of Pascal's triangle sums to $2^n$.

Row $8$: $2^8=256$.
Check: $1+8+28+56+70+56+28+8+1=256$. ✓

Q: What is the coefficient of $x^3$ in the expansion of $(x+2)^5$?
L: 1
A: 40
W: 10
W: 80
W: 20
W: 32
S: Binomial Theorem: $(x+2)^5=\sum_{k}\binom5k x^k\,2^{5-k}$.
For $x^3$ we need $k=3$, so the term is $\binom53x^3\cdot2^{2}$.
$\binom53=10$ and $2^2=4$, so the coefficient is $10\cdot4=40$.
(Forgetting the power of $2$ gives $10$; using $2^3$ instead of $2^2$ gives $80$.)

In general $\sum_k k\binom nk=n\,2^{n-1}$.
