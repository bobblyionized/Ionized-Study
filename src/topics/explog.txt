=== LESSONS ===
<script type="text/plain" id="L-exp-rules">
<h2>Exponent Rules &amp; Equations</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Laws</h4><ul>
<li>$a^ma^n=a^{m+n}$, $\quad\frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n}$, $\quad(a^m)^n=a^{mn}$, $\quad(ab)^n=a^nb^n$.</li>
<li>$a^{-n}=\frac1{a^n}$, $\quad a^{m/n}=\left(\sqrt[n]a\right)^m$, $\quad a^0=1$.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Solving</h3>
<ul><li><b>Match the bases:</b> $4^x=8^{x-1}$ becomes $2^{2x}=2^{3x-3}$.</li>
<li><b>Factor out the smallest power:</b> $2^{x+2}-2^x=2^x(4-1)$.</li>
<li><b>Quadratic in disguise:</b> $4^x-3\cdot2^x-4=0$ with $u=2^x$. Remember $u>0$.</li>
<li><b>Swapping roles:</b> if $3^a=5$ and $5^b=9$, then $3^{ab}=9$, so $ab=2$.</li></ul>
<h3>Comparing powers</h3>
<p>Rewrite with a common exponent: $2^{100}=(2^5)^{20}=32^{20}$ and $3^{60}=27^{20}$, so $2^{100}$ is larger.</p>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-log-prop">
<h2>Logarithm Properties &amp; Change of Base</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Rules</h4><ul>
<li>$\log_b x=y\iff b^y=x$.</li>
<li>$\log_b(xy)=\log_bx+\log_by$, $\quad\log_b\frac xy=\log_bx-\log_by$, $\quad\log_bx^k=k\log_bx$.</li>
<li>Change of base: $\log_ab=\frac{\log b}{\log a}$. So $\log_ab\cdot\log_bc=\log_ac$ and $\log_ab=\frac1{\log_ba}$.</li>
<li>$\log_{a^m}b^n=\frac nm\log_ab$; for example $\log_432=\log_{2^2}2^5=\frac52$.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Patterns</h3>
<ul><li>Sums of logs become logs of products, which often telescope: $\sum\log_2\frac{k+1}{k}=\log_2\frac{n+1}{1}$.</li>
<li>$\frac1{\log_a N}+\frac1{\log_b N}=\log_N a+\log_N b=\log_N(ab)$.</li>
<li>Express everything in terms of $\log 2$ and $\log 3$ (or given values $x, y$): $\log 12=2\log2+\log3$.</li></ul>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-log-eq">
<h2>Log Equations &amp; Number of Digits</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Solving log equations</h4><ul>
<li>Combine the logs into one, rewrite in exponential form, and solve.</li>
<li><b>Check the domain:</b> every log argument must be positive (and bases positive and $\ne1$). Discard extraneous roots.</li>
<li>Quadratic in $\log x$: substitute $t=\log x$.</li>
<li>Nested logs: peel from the outside in: $\log_2(\cdots)=0$ means the inside is $1$.</li>
</ul></div>
<div class="box"><h4>Counting digits</h4>
A positive integer $N$ has $\lfloor\log_{10}N\rfloor+1$ digits. Useful values: $\log_{10}2\approx0.30103$, $\log_{10}3\approx0.47712$.<br>
For products like $2^a5^b$, pair up $10$s first: $2^{30}5^{20}=2^{10}\cdot10^{20}$.</div>
</script>

Q: If $4^x=8^{x-1}$, what is $x$?
L: 1
A: 3
W: 1
W: -3
W: 2
W: 6
S: Rewrite both sides as powers of the same base, $2$: $4=2^2$ and $8=2^3$.
$4^x=(2^2)^x=2^{2x}$ and $8^{x-1}=(2^3)^{x-1}=2^{3x-3}$.
Equal powers of $2$ have equal exponents: $2x=3x-3$, so $x=3$.
Check: $4^3=64$ and $8^2=64$. ✓
---
Q: What is $27^{2/3}$?
L: 1
A: 9
W: 18
W: 3
W: 6
W: 81
S: A fractional exponent $\frac mn$ means "take the $n$th root, then raise to the $m$th power": $a^{m/n}=\left(\sqrt[n]a\right)^m$.
$\sqrt[3]{27}=3$, since $3^3=27$.
So $27^{2/3}=3^2=9$.
(Taking the root first keeps the numbers small; $(27^2)^{1/3}=\sqrt[3]{729}=9$ gives the same answer.)
---
Q: If $2^{x+2}-2^x=48$, what is $x$?
L: 1
A: 4
W: 3
W: 5
W: 6
W: 8
S: Factor out the smaller power: $2^{x+2}=2^x\cdot2^2=4\cdot2^x$.
So $2^{x+2}-2^x=4\cdot2^x-2^x=3\cdot2^x$.
$3\cdot2^x=48$ gives $2^x=16=2^4$, so $x=4$.
Check: $2^6-2^4=64-16=48$. ✓

Q: Which of these is largest?
L: 2
A: 2^{100}
W: 3^{60}
W: 5^{40}
W: 6^{30}
W: 7^{20}
S: To compare powers with different bases and exponents, rewrite them all with the <b>same exponent</b>. Every exponent here is a multiple of $10$... and of $20$ except $30$, so use $20$ and handle $6^{30}$ separately.
• $2^{100}=(2^5)^{20}=32^{20}$
• $3^{60}=(3^3)^{20}=27^{20}$
• $5^{40}=(5^2)^{20}=25^{20}$
• $7^{20}$
• $6^{30}=(6^3)^{10}=216^{10}$, while $2^{100}=(2^{10})^{10}=1024^{10}$.
The base $32$ beats $27$, $25$ and $7$, and $1024^{10}\gt216^{10}$, so $2^{100}$ is the largest.

Q: What real $x$ satisfies $4^x-3\cdot2^x-4=0$?
L: 2
A: 2
W: 1
W: 4
W: -1
W: 0
S: Notice $4^x=(2^x)^2$. Substitute $u=2^x$, which is always positive.
The equation becomes $u^2-3u-4=0$, which factors as $(u-4)(u+1)=0$.
$u=-1$ is impossible because $2^x\gt0$. So $u=4$, i.e. $2^x=4$ and $x=2$.
Check: $16-3\cdot4-4=0$. ✓

Q: What is $\dfrac{2^{10}+2^{10}}{2^9}$?
L: 1
A: 4
W: 2
W: 8
W: 2^{11}
W: 1
S: The numerator is $2^{10}+2^{10}=2\cdot2^{10}=2^{11}$ (two copies of the same number).
Divide: $\dfrac{2^{11}}{2^9}=2^{11-9}=2^2=4$.
(A common slip is to add exponents, $2^{10}+2^{10}\ne2^{20}$.)

Q: If $3^a=5$ and $5^b=9$, what is $ab$?
L: 2
A: 2
W: 1
W: 3
W: 45
W: \frac{9}{5}
S: Chain the two equations: $3^{ab}=(3^a)^b$.
Since $3^a=5$, this is $5^b$, and $5^b=9$.
So $3^{ab}=9=3^2$, which forces $ab=2$.
(You never need the individual values $a=\log_35$ and $b=\log_59$.)
---
Q: How many real solutions does $2^x=x^2$ have?
L: 3
A: 3
W: 2
W: 1
W: 4
W: 0
S: Look for solutions in three regions.
• $x=2$: $2^2=4=2^2$. ✓ $\;x=4$: $2^4=16=4^2$. ✓ These are the two positive solutions (the curves cross at $2$ and again at $4$; beyond $4$, $2^x$ grows faster).
• $x\lt0$: as $x$ goes from $-\infty$ up to $0$, $2^x$ rises from near $0$ to $1$, while $x^2$ falls from huge values to $0$. A rising curve and a falling curve cross exactly once, somewhere near $x\approx-0.77$.
Total: $3$ real solutions.

W: 3
S: Let $\log_4 32=x$, which means $4^x=32$.
Write both as powers of $2$: $2^{2x}=2^5$, so $2x=5$ and $x=\frac52$.
Check: $4^{5/2}=(\sqrt4)^5=2^5=32$. ✓

Q: What is $\log_2 3\cdot\log_3 4\cdot\log_4 8$?
L: 1
A: 3
W: 24
W: 1
W: 6
W: 9
S: Change of base: $\log_ab=\dfrac{\ln b}{\ln a}$.
The product is $\dfrac{\ln3}{\ln2}\cdot\dfrac{\ln4}{\ln3}\cdot\dfrac{\ln8}{\ln4}$; every middle factor cancels (it telescopes), leaving $\dfrac{\ln8}{\ln2}=\log_28$.
$\log_28=3$.

Q: If $\log_a 2=x$ and $\log_a 3=y$, what is $\log_a 12$?
L: 1
A: 2x+y
W: x+y
W: 2xy
W: x^2y
W: x+2y
S: Factor $12$ into the given pieces: $12=2^2\cdot3$.
Log rules: $\log_a(MN)=\log_aM+\log_aN$ and $\log_a(M^k)=k\log_aM$.
So $\log_a12=\log_a2^2+\log_a3=2\log_a2+\log_a3=2x+y$.

Q: What is $\log_{10}2+\log_{10}50$?
L: 1
A: 2
W: 1
W: 52

W: 100
S: Product rule: $\log_{10}2+\log_{10}50=\log_{10}(2\cdot50)=\log_{10}100$.
$10^2=100$, so the answer is $2$.
(Logs of a sum don't split, but a sum of logs is the log of a product.)

Q: What is $\log_2\!\left(1+\frac11\right)+\log_2\!\left(1+\frac12\right)+\cdots+\log_2\!\left(1+\frac1{31}\right)$?
L: 2

W: 4
W: 6

W: 32

$\log_232=5$.

Q: What real $x$ satisfies $\log_2 x+\log_2(x-2)=3$?
L: 1
A: 4
W: -2
W: 2
W: 8
W: 6
S: Combine the logs: $\log_2x+\log_2(x-2)=\log_2\big(x(x-2)\big)=3$, so $x(x-2)=2^3=8$.
$x^2-2x-8=0$ factors as $(x-4)(x+2)=0$, giving $x=4$ or $x=-2$.
Domain check: both $\log_2x$ and $\log_2(x-2)$ need positive inputs, so $x\gt2$. Reject $-2$.
$x=4$. Check: $\log_24+\log_22=2+1=3$. ✓

W: 30
W: 100
W: 32
W: 29

$\log_{10}2^{100}=100\log_{10}2\approx100(0.30103)=30.103$.
So $2^{100}$ has $\lfloor30.103\rfloor+1=31$ digits. (It's about $1.27\times10^{30}$.)

Q: What real $x$ satisfies $\log_3(x+1)-\log_3(x-1)=1$?
L: 1
A: 2
W: 3
W: 1
W: 4
W: \frac{1}{2}
S: Quotient rule: $\log_3(x+1)-\log_3(x-1)=\log_3\dfrac{x+1}{x-1}=1$.
So $\dfrac{x+1}{x-1}=3^1=3$, giving $x+1=3x-3$ and $x=2$.
Domain: we need $x-1\gt0$; $x=2$ works. Check: $\log_33-\log_31=1-0=1$. ✓
---
Q: What is the product of all real solutions of $(\log_2 x)^2-5\log_2 x+6=0$?
L: 2
A: 32
W: 12
W: 6
W: 24
W: 64
S: Substitute $u=\log_2x$: $u^2-5u+6=0$, i.e. $(u-2)(u-3)=0$.
So $\log_2x=2$ or $3$, giving $x=4$ or $x=8$.
Product: $4\cdot8=32$.
Shortcut: by Vieta the two values of $u$ add to $5$, and $x_1x_2=2^{u_1}\cdot2^{u_2}=2^{u_1+u_2}=2^5=32$.

Q: If $2^x=10$, what is $\lfloor x\rfloor$?
L: 1
A: 3
W: 4
W: 2
W: 5
W: 10
S: $\lfloor x\rfloor$ is the integer just below $x=\log_210$.
Trap $10$ between consecutive powers of $2$: $2^3=8\lt10\lt16=2^4$.
So $3\lt x\lt4$ and $\lfloor x\rfloor=3$. (In fact $x\approx3.32$.)

Q: How many digits does $5^{20}\cdot2^{30}$ have?
L: 2

W: 30
W: 50
S: Pair each $5$ with a $2$ to make $10$s: $5^{20}\cdot2^{30}=(5\cdot2)^{20}\cdot2^{10}=10^{20}\cdot1024$.
$1024$ has $4$ digits, and multiplying by $10^{20}$ appends $20$ zeros.
Total: $4+20=24$ digits.

Q: What real $x$ satisfies $\log_2(\log_3(\log_4 x))=0$?
L: 2
A: 64
W: 12
W: 81
W: 16
W: 256
S: Peel off the logs from the outside in, using $\log_by=c\iff y=b^c$.
$\log_2(\cdots)=0\Rightarrow\log_3(\log_4x)=2^0=1$.
$\log_3(\log_4x)=1\Rightarrow\log_4x=3^1=3$.
$\log_4x=3\Rightarrow x=4^3=64$.
