<script type="text/plain" id="L-quad-solve">
<h2>Solving Quadratics</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Three methods</h4><ul>
<li><b>Factoring:</b> find two numbers with product $ac$ and sum $b$. Use this whenever the roots are rational.</li>
<li><b>Completing the square:</b> $x^2+bx=\left(x+\frac b2\right)^2-\frac{b^2}4$.</li>
<li><b>Quadratic formula:</b> $x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}$.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Equations that hide a quadratic</h3>
<ul><li><b>Quadratic in $x^2$:</b> $x^4-13x^2+36=0$; set $u=x^2$, so $u=4,9$ and $x=\pm2,\pm3$.</li>
<li><b>$x+\frac1x$:</b> multiply through by $x$.</li>
<li><b>Radical equations:</b> isolate the root and square, then <b>check every answer</b>. Squaring creates extraneous roots.</li>
<li><b>Never divide by an expression that could be $0$:</b> $(x-3)^2=2(x-3)$ has the solutions $3$ <i>and</i> $5$.</li></ul>
<h3>Integer-root counting</h3>
<p>If $x^2+bx+c$ has integer roots $r,s$, then $rs=c$ and $b=-(r+s)$. Count factor pairs of $c$, including negative pairs.</p>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example.</b> Find the sum of all real $x$ with $(x^2-5x+5)^{x^2-9x+20}=1$.
<details><summary>Solution</summary>A power equals 1 in three ways. Base $1$: $x=1,4$. Exponent $0$ with base $\ne0$: $x=4,5$. Base $-1$ with an even exponent: $x=2,3$ (the exponents are 6 and 2). The set $\{1,2,3,4,5\}$ sums to $15$.</details></div>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-quad-disc">
<h2>Discriminant &amp; Nature of Roots</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>$\Delta=b^2-4ac$ for $ax^2+bx+c=0$</h4><ul>
<li>$\Delta>0$: two distinct real roots.</li>
<li>$\Delta=0$: one repeated real root, so the parabola touches the $x$-axis (tangency).</li>
<li>$\Delta\lt 0$: no real roots (two complex conjugate roots).</li>
<li>With integer coefficients, the roots are rational exactly when $\Delta$ is a perfect square.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Tangency</h3>
<p>A line and a parabola are tangent exactly when substituting one into the other gives a quadratic with $\Delta=0$.</p>
<h3>Watch the leading coefficient</h3>
<p>"Exactly one solution" for $kx^2+4x+1=0$ includes $k=0$ (the equation becomes linear) as well as $\Delta=0$.</p>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example.</b> For how many positive integers $n\le50$ does $x^2+x-n=0$ have integer roots?
<details><summary>Solution</summary>We need $1+4n$ to be an odd perfect square $(2k+1)^2$, so $n=k(k+1)$: $2,6,12,20,30,42$. That is $6$ values.</details></div>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-quad-vertex">
<h2>Vertex Form &amp; Optimization</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Vertex</h4><ul>
<li>$y=ax^2+bx+c$ has its vertex at $x=-\dfrac{b}{2a}$, and its extreme value is $c-\dfrac{b^2}{4a}$.</li>
<li>Vertex form: $y=a(x-h)^2+k$ with vertex $(h,k)$. If $a>0$, $k$ is the minimum; if $a\lt 0$, the maximum.</li>
<li>The axis of symmetry is $x=h$, and the roots are symmetric about it.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Classic optimizations</h3>
<ul><li>Fixed sum $S$: the product $x(S-x)$ is largest at $x=\frac S2$, giving $\frac{S^2}{4}$.</li>
<li>Rectangle with fixed perimeter: a square maximizes the area.</li>
<li>Fence on three sides (a wall or river on the fourth): maximize $x(L-2x)$, so $x=\frac L4$.</li>
<li>Projectile $h=-16t^2+vt+h_0$: the peak is at $t=\frac{v}{32}$.</li>
<li><b>Products of four factors:</b> $(x-1)(x-3)(x-5)(x-7)$. Pair the outer and inner factors to get a common quadratic $u$, then the product is $(u-4)(u+4)=u^2-16$.</li></ul>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-quad-vieta">
<h2>Vieta for Quadratics</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>For $ax^2+bx+c=0$ with roots $r,s$</h4>
$r+s=-\dfrac ba,\qquad rs=\dfrac ca$</div>
<h3>Symmetric expressions</h3>
<ul><li>$r^2+s^2=(r+s)^2-2rs$</li>
<li>$\frac1r+\frac1s=\frac{r+s}{rs}$</li>
<li>$(r-s)^2=(r+s)^2-4rs$, so $|r-s|=\frac{\sqrt\Delta}{|a|}$</li>
<li>$r^3+s^3=(r+s)^3-3rs(r+s)$</li>
<li>Power sums $p_n=r^n+s^n$ satisfy $p_n=(r+s)p_{n-1}-rs\,p_{n-2}$.</li></ul>
<h3>Building new equations</h3>
<p>For roots $r+k, s+k$: the new sum is $(r+s)+2k$ and the new product is $rs+k(r+s)+k^2$. The new monic equation is $x^2-(\text{sum})x+\text{product}=0$.</p>
<div class="tip"><h4>AMC tip</h4>You almost never need to find the roots themselves. If the roots are ugly (irrational), Vieta is definitely the intended path.</div>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-quad-sys">
<h2>Quadratic Systems &amp; Intersections</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Strategies</h4><ul>
<li><b>Substitute</b> a linear equation into a quadratic and solve the resulting quadratic. Its discriminant counts the intersection points (0, 1 or 2).</li>
<li><b>Symmetric systems:</b> use $s=x+y$, $p=xy$. Then $x,y$ are the roots of $t^2-st+p=0$.</li>
<li><b>Add or subtract equations</b> to create a factorable expression: $x^2+xy=15,\ y^2+xy=10$ adds to $(x+y)^2=25$.</li>
<li><b>Difference of squares:</b> $x^2-y^2=24$ with $x-y=4$ gives $x+y=6$.</li>
<li>Curve–curve intersections: eliminate one variable, then count the valid solutions of the other. Each $y$ may give 0, 1 or 2 values of $x$.</li>
</ul></div>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example.</b> How many points do $y=x^2-4$ and $x^2+y^2=16$ share?
<details><summary>Solution</summary>$x^2=y+4$, so $y^2+y-12=0$ and $y=3$ or $-4$. $y=3$ gives $x=\pm\sqrt7$ (2 points); $y=-4$ gives $x=0$ (1 point). Total $3$.</details></div>
</script>
