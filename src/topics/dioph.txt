=== LESSONS ===
<script type="text/plain" id="L-dioph-lin">
<h2>Linear Diophantine Equations</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Facts for $ax+by=c$</h4><ul>
<li>It has integer solutions exactly when $g=\gcd(a,b)$ divides $c$.</li>
<li>From one solution $(x_0,y_0)$, all solutions are $x=x_0+\frac bg t$, $y=y_0-\frac ag t$.</li>
<li>To count nonnegative or positive solutions, find the range of $t$, or list the values of one variable in the right residue class.</li>
</ul></div>
<div class="box"><h4>Chicken McNugget theorem</h4>
For coprime $a,b$: the largest amount that <b>cannot</b> be made from $a$'s and $b$'s is $ab-a-b$, and there are $\frac{(a-1)(b-1)}{2}$ unreachable amounts.</div>
<h3>Technique: use residues</h3>
<p>For $3x+5y=100$: $5y\equiv100\pmod3$ gives $y\equiv2\pmod3$. List $y=2,5,8,\dots$ while $5y\le100$.</p>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-dioph-sfft">
<h2>Simon's Favorite Factoring Trick</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>The trick</h4>
$xy+ax+by=c\iff(x+b)(y+a)=c+ab$. Add the constant that completes the rectangle, then count factor pairs.</div>
<h3>Variants</h3>
<ul><li>$xy=2x+3y$ becomes $(x-3)(y-2)=6$.</li>
<li>$2xy+x+y=c$: multiply by 2 and add 1 to get $(2x+1)(2y+1)=2c+1$.</li>
<li>$\frac1x+\frac1y=\frac1n$ becomes $(x-n)(y-n)=n^2$, with $d(n^2)$ positive solutions.</li>
<li>Area $=$ perimeter for a rectangle: $ab=2a+2b$ gives $(a-2)(b-2)=4$.</li></ul>
<h3>Checking negative factors</h3>
<p>Both factors could be negative. Check whether that gives positive $x,y$ (it usually does not). Count ordered pairs unless the problem says otherwise.</p>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-dioph-bound">
<h2>Bounding &amp; Case Analysis</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Tools</h4><ul>
<li><b>Parity and mod arguments:</b> $x^2-y^2$ is never $\equiv2\pmod4$; squares are $0,1\pmod4$; sums of factorials eventually end in a fixed digit.</li>
<li><b>Complete the square</b> to reach a difference of squares: $x^2=y^2+2y+13$ becomes $x^2-(y+1)^2=12$.</li>
<li><b>Order the variables</b> ($x\le y\le z$) to bound the smallest one. For $\frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z=1$ this gives $x\le3$.</li>
<li><b>Factor powers:</b> $2^a-2^b=2^b(2^{a-b}-1)$; match the power of 2 against the odd part.</li>
<li>Growth: exponentials and factorials outgrow polynomials, so only small cases need checking.</li>
</ul></div>
<p>Sums of two squares: list the squares below $N$ and check. $50=1+49=25+25$.</p>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-dioph-floor">
<h2>Floor Function Equations</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Method</h4><ul>
<li>Write $x=n+f$ with $n=\lfloor x\rfloor$ an integer and $0\le f\lt 1$.</li>
<li>Equations like $x+\lfloor x\rfloor=v$ become $2n+f=v$, which forces $n=\lfloor v/2\rfloor$ (and requires $v-2n\lt 1$).</li>
<li>For $x^2=g(\lfloor x\rfloor)$: try each integer $n$, compute the candidate $x$, then check that $\lfloor x\rfloor=n$.</li>
<li>Periodic behavior: $\lfloor\frac n2\rfloor+\lfloor\frac n3\rfloor+\dots$ depends on $n$ modulo the lcm. Check one period.</li>
</ul></div>
<p>Helpful identities: $\lfloor x+k\rfloor=\lfloor x\rfloor+k$ for integer $k$; $\lceil x\rceil=-\lfloor-x\rfloor$; $\lfloor x\rfloor+\lfloor-x\rfloor=-1$ for non-integers.</p>
</script>
