=== LESSONS ===
<script type="text/plain" id="L-conic-para">
<h2>Parabolas</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Focus and directrix</h4><ul>
<li>A parabola is the set of points equidistant from a <b>focus</b> and a <b>directrix</b>.</li>
<li>$x^2=4py$: vertex $(0,0)$, focus $(0,p)$, directrix $y=-p$.</li>
<li>$y^2=4px$: focus $(p,0)$, directrix $x=-p$.</li>
<li>$y=ax^2$ means $x^2=\frac1ay$, so $p=\frac1{4a}$.</li>
<li>Latus rectum (the chord through the focus parallel to the directrix) has length $4|p|$.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Useful moves</h3>
<ul><li>Complete the square to find the vertex, then shift the focus and directrix along with it.</li>
<li>The distance from a point on $x^2=4py$ to the focus equals its distance to the directrix: $y+p$. This turns distance problems into one subtraction.</li></ul>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-conic-ell">
<h2>Ellipses &amp; Hyperbolas</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Ellipse $\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1$ with $a>b$</h4><ul>
<li>Foci at $(\pm c,0)$ with $c^2=a^2-b^2$.</li>
<li>For every point on it, the sum of the distances to the foci is $2a$ (the major axis).</li>
<li>Area $\pi ab$; eccentricity $e=\frac ca\lt 1$.</li>
</ul></div>
<div class="box"><h4>Hyperbola $\frac{x^2}{a^2}-\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1$</h4><ul>
<li>Foci at $(\pm c,0)$ with $c^2=a^2+b^2$.</li>
<li>The difference of the distances to the foci is $2a$.</li>
<li>Asymptotes $y=\pm\frac bax$.</li>
</ul></div>
<p>To read off $a$ and $b$, first divide the equation so its right side is $1$: $4x^2+9y^2=36$ becomes $\frac{x^2}9+\frac{y^2}4=1$.</p>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-conic-param">
<h2>Parametric Equations &amp; Vectors</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Parametric curves</h4><ul>
<li><b>Eliminate the parameter:</b> solve one equation for $t$ and substitute, or use an identity ($\cos^2t+\sin^2t=1$, $\cos2t=2\cos^2t-1$).</li>
<li>$x=a\cos t,\ y=b\sin t$ traces the ellipse $\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1$; with $a=b$ it is a circle.</li>
<li>$x=t^2,\ y=2t$ traces the parabola $y^2=4x$.</li>
<li>Watch the range: $x=\cos t$ only gives $-1\le x\le1$.</li>
</ul></div>
<div class="box"><h4>Vectors</h4><ul>
<li>Dot product $\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v=u_1v_1+u_2v_2=|\mathbf u||\mathbf v|\cos\theta$. It is $0$ exactly when the vectors are perpendicular.</li>
<li>Parallelogram spanned by $(a,b)$ and $(c,d)$: area $|ad-bc|$; the triangle is half that.</li>
<li>Projection of $\mathbf u$ onto $\mathbf v$ has length $\frac{|\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v|}{|\mathbf v|}$.</li>
</ul></div>
</script>

Q: What is the $y$-coordinate of the focus of the parabola $y=\dfrac{x^2}{8}$?
L: 1
A: 2
W: 8
W: 4
W: \frac{1}{8}
W: \frac{1}{2}
S: A parabola $x^2=4py$ (vertex at the origin, opening up) has its focus at $(0,p)$ and directrix $y=-p$.
Rewrite $y=\frac{x^2}{8}$ as $x^2=8y$. Matching $4p=8$ gives $p=2$.
So the focus is $(0,2)$, and its $y$-coordinate is $2$.

Q: What is the length of the latus rectum of the parabola $y=2x^2$?
L: 2
A: \frac{1}{2}
W: 2
W: \frac{1}{8}
W: 4
W: 1
S: Put it in standard form: $y=2x^2\iff x^2=\frac12y$, so $4p=\frac12$ and $p=\frac18$.
The latus rectum is the chord through the focus parallel to the directrix. At $y=p$: $x^2=4p\cdot p$, so $x=\pm2p$ and its length is $4p$.
Length $=4p=\frac12$.

Q: A point on the parabola $x^2=4y$ is at distance $10$ from the focus. What is the absolute value of its $x$-coordinate?
L: 2
A: 6
W: 9
W: 3
W: 10
W: 8
S: $x^2=4y$ has $4p=4$, $p=1$: focus $(0,1)$, directrix $y=-1$.
Every point on a parabola is equally far from the focus and the directrix. The distance from $(x,y)$ to the directrix is $y-(-1)=y+1$.
So $y+1=10$, $y=9$, and $x^2=4\cdot9=36$, giving $|x|=6$.

(Alternatively $h=-\frac{b}{2a}=3$ and $k=9-18+13=4$.)

Q: What is the distance between the foci of the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{25}+\dfrac{y^2}{9}=1$?
L: 1
A: 8
W: 4
W: 6
W: 10
W: 16
S: For an ellipse $\frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1$ with $a\gt b$, the foci are at $(\pm c,0)$ where $c^2=a^2-b^2$.
$c^2=25-9=16$, so $c=4$.
The foci $(-4,0)$ and $(4,0)$ are $8$ apart.
---
Q: What is the area enclosed by the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{16}+\dfrac{y^2}{9}=1$?
L: 1
A: 12\pi
W: 25\pi
W: 7\pi
W: 144\pi
W: 24\pi
S: An ellipse with semi-axes $a$ and $b$ has area $\pi ab$ (a circle of radius $r$ is the case $a=b=r$).
Here $a^2=16$, $b^2=9$, so $a=4$, $b=3$, and the area is $\pi\cdot4\cdot3=12\pi$.
---
Q: The hyperbola $\dfrac{x^2}{9}-\dfrac{y^2}{16}=1$ has foci at $(\pm c,0)$. What is $c$?
L: 1
A: 5
W: \sqrt{7}
W: 7
W: 4
W: 25
S: For a hyperbola $\frac{x^2}{a^2}-\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1$, the foci satisfy $c^2=a^2+b^2$ (a plus sign, unlike the ellipse).
$c^2=9+16=25$, so $c=5$.
---
Q: What is the positive slope of an asymptote of $\dfrac{x^2}{4}-\dfrac{y^2}{9}=1$?
L: 1
A: \frac{3}{2}
W: \frac{2}{3}
W: \frac{9}{4}
W: \frac{4}{9}
W: 3
S: The asymptotes of $\frac{x^2}{a^2}-\frac{y^2}{b^2}=1$ are $y=\pm\frac bax$ (set the right side to $0$: $\frac{y^2}{b^2}=\frac{x^2}{a^2}$).
$a=2$, $b=3$, so the positive slope is $\frac32$.
---
Q: For any point on the ellipse $\dfrac{x^2}{25}+\dfrac{y^2}{16}=1$, what is the sum of its distances to the two foci?
L: 1
A: 10
W: 8
W: 6
W: 5
W: 20
S: By definition, an ellipse is the set of points whose distances to the two foci add to a constant, and that constant is the major axis length $2a$.
(At the vertex $(a,0)$: the distances are $a-c$ and $a+c$, which add to $2a$.)
Here $a^2=25$, so the sum is $2a=10$.
---
Q: An ellipse has foci $(\pm4,0)$ and major axis length $10$. What is the length of its minor axis?
L: 2
A: 6
W: 8
W: 3
W: 5
W: 4
S: Major axis $10$ means $a=5$. Foci at $(\pm4,0)$ means $c=4$.
For an ellipse, $b^2=a^2-c^2=25-16=9$, so $b=3$.
The minor axis has length $2b=6$.

Q: What is the area enclosed by $4x^2+9y^2=36$?

So $a=3$, $b=2$, and the area is $\pi ab=6\pi$.

Q: A curve is given by $x=2t+1$, $y=t-3$. What is its slope?
L: 1
A: \frac{1}{2}
W: 2
W: -\frac{1}{2}
W: 7
W: 1
S: Eliminate $t$: from $y=t-3$, $t=y+3$. Then $x=2(y+3)+1=2y+7$, i.e. $y=\frac12x-\frac72$.
Slope $\frac12$. Shortcut: as $t$ increases by $1$, $x$ increases by $2$ and $y$ by $1$, so rise/run $=\frac12$.

Q: What is the area enclosed by the curve $x=3\cos t$, $y=3\sin t$?
L: 1
A: 9\pi
W: 3\pi
W: 6\pi
W: 9
W: 18\pi
S: $x^2+y^2=9\cos^2t+9\sin^2t=9(\cos^2t+\sin^2t)=9$.
So the curve is the circle of radius $3$ centered at the origin, enclosing area $\pi\cdot3^2=9\pi$.

Q: What is the dot product of $(3,4)$ and $(-4,3)$?
L: 1
A: 0
W: 25
W: 1
W: -24
W: 24
S: Dot product: multiply matching components and add: $(3)(-4)+(4)(3)=-12+12=0$.
A zero dot product means the vectors are perpendicular (indeed $(-4,3)$ is $(3,4)$ rotated $90^\circ$).

Q: What is the angle between the vectors $(1,0)$ and $(1,1)$, in degrees?
L: 1
A: 45
W: 30
W: 60
W: 90
W: 135
S: $\cos\theta=\dfrac{\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v}{|\mathbf u||\mathbf v|}=\dfrac{1\cdot1+0\cdot1}{1\cdot\sqrt2}=\dfrac1{\sqrt2}$.
So $\theta=45^\circ$. (Geometrically, $(1,1)$ points along the line $y=x$, at $45^\circ$ to the $x$-axis.)

Q: The curve $x=\cos t$, $y=\cos2t$ lies on a parabola. What is the minimum value of $y$ on this curve?
L: 2
A: -1
W: 0
W: 1

S: Double-angle identity: $\cos2t=2\cos^2t-1$, so $y=2x^2-1$: a parabola.

On it, $y$ is smallest at $x=0$: $y=-1$ (and largest, $1$, at $x=\pm1$).
