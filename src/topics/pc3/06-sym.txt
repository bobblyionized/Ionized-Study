<h2>3.4.A Symmetry</h2>
<p>A symmetric graph is determined by half of itself: draw one half, reflect it, and you're done. There are three symmetries to know, each with a geometric description and an algebraic test.</p>

<h3>1. The three symmetries</h3>
<table class="t">
<tr><th>Symmetry</th><th>Geometric meaning</th><th>Point rule</th><th>Algebraic test</th></tr>
<tr><td>about the <b>$y$-axis</b></td><td>the left half is the mirror image of the right half</td><td>$(x,y)$ on graph $\Rightarrow(-x,y)$ on graph</td><td>replacing $x$ by $-x$ gives an equivalent equation</td></tr>
<tr><td>about the <b>$x$-axis</b></td><td>the bottom half mirrors the top half</td><td>$(x,y)\Rightarrow(x,-y)$</td><td>replacing $y$ by $-y$ gives an equivalent equation</td></tr>
<tr><td>about the <b>origin</b></td><td>rotating the graph $180^\circ$ about $(0,0)$ leaves it unchanged</td><td>$(x,y)\Rightarrow(-x,-y)$</td><td>replacing $x$ by $-x$ <b>and</b> $y$ by $-y$ gives an equivalent equation</td></tr></table>

<div class="lplot" data-f="0.25*x*x*x*x-2*x*x+1" data-x="-4,4" data-y="-4,4" data-w="170" data-cap="$y$-axis symmetry"></div>

<h3>2. Using the algebraic tests</h3>
<ul><li>$y=x^4-3x^2$: replace $x$ by $-x$: $y=(-x)^4-3(-x)^2=x^4-3x^2$, the same equation. Symmetric about the $y$-axis.</li>
<li>$x=y^2+1$: replace $y$ by $-y$: $x=(-y)^2+1=y^2+1$, the same. Symmetric about the $x$-axis.</li>
<li>$y=x^3-x$: replace both: $-y=(-x)^3-(-x)=-x^3+x$, i.e. $y=x^3-x$. Symmetric about the origin.</li>
<li>$x^2+y^2=25$: every test works. A circle centered at the origin has all three symmetries.</li>
<li>$xy=4$: replacing both gives $(-x)(-y)=4$, the same; replacing only one gives $-xy=4$, different. Origin symmetry only.</li></ul>
<div class="tip"><h4>Any two imply the third</h4>If a graph has two of the three symmetries, it has all three. For example, reflecting across the $x$-axis and then the $y$-axis is the same as rotating $180^\circ$ about the origin.</div>

<h3>3. Even and odd functions</h3>
<div class="box"><ul>
<li>$f$ is <b>even</b> if $f(-x)=f(x)$ for every $x$ in its domain. Its graph is symmetric about the $y$-axis.</li>
<li>$f$ is <b>odd</b> if $f(-x)=-f(x)$ for every $x$ in its domain. Its graph is symmetric about the origin.</li></ul></div>
<ul><li>A polynomial with <b>only even powers</b> of $x$ (a constant counts as $x^0$) is even: $3x^4-x^2+7$.</li>
<li>A polynomial with <b>only odd powers</b> is odd: $x^5-2x^3+x$.</li>
<li>Mixing them usually gives <b>neither</b>: $x^3+1$ has $f(-1)=0$ but $f(1)=2$, and $0$ is neither $2$ nor $-2$.</li>
<li>$|x|$, $\sqrt{x^2+1}$, $\frac{1}{x^2}$ are even; $\frac1x$, $\sqrt[3]{x}$, $\frac{x}{x^2+1}$ are odd.</li>
<li>Products follow sign rules: even·even and odd·odd are even; even·odd is odd. Compositions: an even function of anything symmetric stays even.</li>
<li>If $f$ is odd and $0$ is in its domain, then $f(0)=-f(0)$, so $f(0)=0$.</li>
<li>The graph of a <b>function</b> can't be symmetric about the $x$-axis (unless it's $y=0$), because $(x,y)$ and $(x,-y)$ would violate the Vertical Line Test.</li></ul>

<h3>4. A classic trick</h3>
<p>Let $f(x)=ax^7+bx^3+cx-5$ and suppose $f(-7)=7$. Find $f(7)$. You can't find $a,b,c$, but $g(x)=f(x)+5=ax^7+bx^3+cx$ is odd. So $g(7)=-g(-7)=-(7+5)=-12$, and $f(7)=g(7)-5=-17$.</p>

<h3>Worked examples</h3>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example 1.</b> A graph is symmetric about the origin and contains $(3,-2)$. What other point must it contain?
<details><summary>Solution</summary>Origin symmetry sends $(x,y)$ to $(-x,-y)$: the point $(-3,2)$.</details></div>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example 2.</b> Is $f(x)=\dfrac{x^3}{x^2-4}$ even, odd, or neither?
<details><summary>Solution</summary>$f(-x)=\dfrac{(-x)^3}{(-x)^2-4}=\dfrac{-x^3}{x^2-4}=-f(x)$, so $f$ is odd. (Odd numerator over even denominator.)</details></div>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example 3.</b> Is $g(x)=x^2+x$ even, odd, or neither?
<details><summary>Solution</summary>$g(-x)=x^2-x$. This is neither $g(x)=x^2+x$ nor $-g(x)=-x^2-x$ (test $x=1$: $g(-1)=0$, $g(1)=2$). So $g$ is <b>neither</b>.</details></div>
<div class="warn"><h4>Common traps</h4><ul>
<li>"Odd" and "even" are not opposites: most functions are neither.</li>
<li>To prove a function is <i>not</i> even or odd, one numerical counterexample is enough. To prove it <i>is</i>, you must check $f(-x)$ algebraically for all $x$.</li>
<li>A constant term spoils oddness: $x^3+1$ is not odd.</li>
</ul></div>

Q: Which equation has a graph that is symmetric about the $x$-axis?
L: 1
A: x=y^2+1
W: y=x^2+1
W: y=x^3
W: y=|x|-2
W: y=\sqrt{x}
S: Test: replace $y$ by $-y$ and see whether the equation is unchanged.
• $x=y^2+1\ \to\ x=(-y)^2+1=y^2+1$. Unchanged, so it's symmetric about the $x$-axis. ✓
• $y=x^2+1\ \to\ -y=x^2+1$, a different equation (and it's symmetric about the $y$-axis instead).
• $y=x^3\to-y=x^3$: different (it has origin symmetry).
• $y=|x|-2$ and $y=\sqrt x$: replacing $y$ by $-y$ changes them.
Note that all four wrong answers are functions of $x$, and a function's graph can't be symmetric about the $x$-axis unless it's $y=0$.

Q: Which function is odd?
L: 1
A: f(x)=\dfrac{x}{x^2+1}
W: f(x)=x^3+1
W: f(x)=x^4-x^2
W: f(x)=|x|
W: f(x)=x^2+x
S: Compute $f(-x)$ and compare with $-f(x)$.
• $\dfrac{-x}{(-x)^2+1}=\dfrac{-x}{x^2+1}=-f(x)$. Odd. ✓
• $x^3+1$: $f(-1)=0$ while $-f(1)=-2$. Not odd (the constant spoils it).
• $x^4-x^2$ and $|x|$: $f(-x)=f(x)$, so they're even, not odd (unless identically zero).
• $x^2+x$: $f(-1)=0$, $-f(1)=-2$. Not odd.

Q: The graph of an equation is symmetric about the origin and contains the point $(3,-2)$. Which point must also be on the graph?
L: 1
A: (-3,\,2)
W: (-3,\,-2)
W: (3,\,2)
W: (-2,\,3)
W: (2,\,-3)
S: Symmetry about the origin means: if $(x,y)$ is on the graph, so is $(-x,-y)$ (a $180^\circ$ rotation about the origin).
$(3,-2)\to(-3,2)$.
($(-3,-2)$ would come from $y$-axis symmetry; $(3,2)$ from $x$-axis symmetry.)

Q: Let $f(x)=ax^7+bx^3+cx-5$, where $a,b,c$ are constants. If $f(-7)=7$, what is $f(7)$?
L: 2
A: -17
W: -7
W: 7
W: 17
W: -12
S: The constants $a,b,c$ can't be found, but we don't need them.
Let $g(x)=f(x)+5=ax^7+bx^3+cx$. Every term has an odd power, so $g$ is <b>odd</b>: $g(-x)=-g(x)$.
$g(-7)=f(-7)+5=12$, so $g(7)=-12$.
Then $f(7)=g(7)-5=-12-5=-17$.
(Answering $-7$ assumes $f$ itself is odd, but the $-5$ prevents that.)

Q: The graph of $x^2+y^2=25$ is symmetric with respect to ...
L: 1
A: \text{the }x\text{-axis, the }y\text{-axis, and the origin}
W: \text{the }y\text{-axis only}
W: \text{the }x\text{-axis only}
W: \text{the origin only}
W: \text{none of these}
S: Replacing $x$ by $-x$: $(-x)^2+y^2=x^2+y^2=25$. Unchanged, so $y$-axis symmetry.
Replacing $y$ by $-y$: also unchanged, so $x$-axis symmetry.
Replacing both: also unchanged, so origin symmetry.
This is the circle of radius $5$ centered at the origin, which has all three symmetries (and many more).

Q: If $f$ is an odd function, $f(2)=5$, and $0$ is in the domain of $f$, what is $f(-2)+f(0)$?
L: 1
A: -5
W: 5
W: 0
W: -10
W: \text{cannot be determined}
S: Odd means $f(-x)=-f(x)$ for all $x$.
$f(-2)=-f(2)=-5$.
For $x=0$: $f(0)=f(-0)=-f(0)$, so $2f(0)=0$ and $f(0)=0$.
Sum: $-5+0=-5$.

Q: The graph of $xy=4$ is symmetric with respect to which of the following?
L: 2
A: \text{the origin only}
W: \text{the }x\text{-axis only}
W: \text{the }y\text{-axis only}
W: \text{all three}
W: \text{none of them}
S: Run the three tests.
• $x\to-x$: $(-x)y=4$, i.e. $xy=-4$. Different, so no $y$-axis symmetry.
• $y\to-y$: $x(-y)=4$, also $xy=-4$. No $x$-axis symmetry.
• Both: $(-x)(-y)=xy=4$. Same! Origin symmetry.
Check with points: $(1,4)$ is on the graph and so is $(-1,-4)$, but $(-1,4)$ is not.
---
Q: If $f$ and $g$ are both odd functions, then the product $h(x)=f(x)g(x)$ is ...
L: 2
A: \text{always even}
W: \text{always odd}
W: \text{always neither}
W: \text{even or odd depending on }f\text{ and }g
W: \text{always zero}
S: Compute $h(-x)=f(-x)\,g(-x)=\big(-f(x)\big)\big(-g(x)\big)=f(x)g(x)=h(x)$.
So $h$ is even, always. Example: $x\cdot x^3=x^4$ is even.
It works like signs: (odd)$\times$(odd)$=$even, just as $(-)\times(-)=(+)$.
---
Q: Is $f(x)=\dfrac{x^3}{x^2-4}$ even, odd, or neither?
L: 1
A: \text{odd}
W: \text{even}
W: \text{neither}
W: \text{both}
W: \text{even on its domain only}
S: $f(-x)=\dfrac{(-x)^3}{(-x)^2-4}=\dfrac{-x^3}{x^2-4}=-\dfrac{x^3}{x^2-4}=-f(x)$.
So $f$ is odd. Notice that the domain (all $x\ne\pm2$) is itself symmetric, which is necessary for the definition to make sense.

Q: Exactly one of these functions is <b>neither</b> even nor odd. Which one?
L: 1
A: f(x)=(x-1)^2
W: f(x)=x^4+2x^2
W: f(x)=x^5-x
W: f(x)=|x|+x^2
W: f(x)=\sqrt[3]{x}
S: • $x^4+2x^2$: only even powers, so even.
• $x^5-x$: only odd powers, so odd.
• $|x|+x^2$: both terms are even, so even.
• $\sqrt[3]{x}$: $\sqrt[3]{-x}=-\sqrt[3]{x}$, so odd.
• $(x-1)^2=x^2-2x+1$ mixes the even terms $x^2,1$ with the odd term $-2x$. Test: $f(-1)=4$, $f(1)=0$. Since $4\ne0$ and $4\ne-0$, it's neither. Its graph is symmetric about the line $x=1$, not the $y$-axis.
