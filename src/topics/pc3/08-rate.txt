<h2>3.6 Rates of Change</h2>
<p>How fast is something changing? A rock dropped from a height falls $d(t)=16t^2$ feet in $t$ seconds. From $t=1$ to $t=3$ it falls $d(3)-d(1)=144-16=128$ feet in $2$ seconds, an average speed of $\frac{128}{2}=64$ feet per second. That calculation (change in output divided by change in input) is the idea of this whole section.</p>

<h3>1. Average rate of change</h3>
<div class="box"><h4>Definition</h4>The <b>average rate of change</b> of $f$ as $x$ changes from $a$ to $b$ is
$$\frac{\text{change in }f(x)}{\text{change in }x}=\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}.$$
Its units are (output units) per (input unit): feet per second, dollars per year, degrees per hour.</div>
<ul><li><b>Speed:</b> an odometer reads $103{,}846$ at 3 P.M. and $104{,}176$ at 9 P.M. Average speed $=\frac{104176-103846}{9-3}=\frac{330}{6}=55$ mph.</li>
<li><b>Data:</b> cell phone users grew from $16.009$ million (1993) to $69.209$ million (1998): $\frac{69.209-16.009}{5}=10.64$ million users per year.</li>
<li><b>Graphs:</b> if the temperature is $40^\circ$ at 4 A.M. and $58^\circ$ at noon, the average rate is $\frac{58-40}{12-4}=2.25^\circ$ per hour. From 3 P.M. ($68^\circ$) to 8 P.M. ($53^\circ$) it is $\frac{53-68}{5}=-3^\circ$ per hour: <b>negative rates mean decreasing</b>.</li></ul>

<h3>2. The geometric meaning: secant lines</h3>
<p>The line through two points $P=(a,f(a))$ and $Q=(b,f(b))$ on a graph is a <b>secant line</b>. Its slope is $\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}$, exactly the average rate of change.</p>
<div class="box">Average rate of change of $f$ from $a$ to $b$ $=$ slope of the secant line from $(a,f(a))$ to $(b,f(b))$.</div>
<div class="lfigs"><div class="lplot" data-f="0.25*x*x;0.25*1+(0.25*25-0.25)/4*(x-1)" data-x="-1,7" data-y="-1,9" data-w="220" data-pts="1,0.25;5,6.25" data-cap="Secant line of $f(x)=\frac14x^2$ from $x=1$ to $x=5$: slope $\frac{6.25-0.25}{4}=1.5$"></div></div>
<p>For a <b>linear</b> function $f(x)=mx+b$ every secant line is the line itself, so the average rate of change between any two inputs is the slope $m$. For example, the pressure $p(T)=0.00411T+1.1213$ always rises $0.00411$ atmospheres per degree.</p>

<h3>3. Small intervals and the difference quotient</h3>
<p>To see what's happening <i>near</i> a point, use short intervals, from $x$ to $x+h$ with $h$ small. The average rate over that interval is</p>
$$\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{(x+h)-x}=\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h},$$
<p>which is the <b>difference quotient</b> from Section 3.2. One algebraic computation handles every $h$ at once. For the rock, from $t=4$ to $t=4+h$:</p>
$$\frac{16(4+h)^2-16(16)}{h}=\frac{128h+16h^2}{h}=128+16h.$$
<p>From $4$ to $4.1$ the average speed is $129.6$ ft/sec; from $4$ to $4.001$ it is $128.016$; from $4$ to $4.00001$ it's $128.00016$.</p>

<h3>4. Instantaneous rate of change</h3>
<p>As the interval shrinks ($h\to0$), the average rates approach a single number: the <b>instantaneous rate of change</b>. For the rock at $t=4$, $128+16h$ approaches $128$, so its speed at the instant $t=4$ is $128$ feet per second. Geometrically, the secant lines approach the <b>tangent line</b> at that point, and the instantaneous rate is the tangent line's slope.</p>
<ul><li>Estimate numerically: compute the average rate on $[a,a+h]$ for $h=0.1,\ 0.01,\ 0.001$ (and also negative $h$) and see what the values approach.</li>
<li>Or compute the difference quotient algebraically, simplify, and then let $h$ be $0$.</li>
<li>For $f(x)=ax^2+bx+c$ the difference quotient is $2ax+b+ah$, so the instantaneous rate at $x$ is $2ax+b$.</li></ul>
<p>This idea, the limit of difference quotients, is the <b>derivative</b>, the starting point of calculus.</p>

<h3>Worked examples</h3>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example 1.</b> A ball's height is $h(t)=-16t^2+64t$ feet. Find its average velocity from $t=1$ to $t=3$, and interpret it.
<details><summary>Solution</summary>$h(1)=48$ and $h(3)=-144+192=48$, so the average velocity is $\frac{48-48}{2}=0$ ft/sec. The ball went up and came back down to the same height, so its net change in height is zero, even though it was moving the whole time.</details></div>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example 2.</b> Find the average rate of change of $f(x)=\sqrt x$ from $4$ to $9$.
<details><summary>Solution</summary>$\frac{\sqrt9-\sqrt4}{9-4}=\frac{3-2}{5}=\frac15$.</details></div>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example 3.</b> Estimate the instantaneous rate of change of $f(x)=x^3$ at $x=2$.
<details><summary>Solution</summary>The difference quotient is $\frac{(2+h)^3-8}{h}=12+6h+h^2$. As $h\to0$ this approaches $\mathbf{12}$. Numerically: $h=0.01$ gives $12.0601$.</details></div>
<div class="warn"><h4>Common traps</h4><ul>
<li>Dividing by the change in <b>output</b> instead of input, or subtracting in opposite orders on top and bottom.</li>
<li>Using the average of the two speeds instead of total change over total time.</li>
<li>Thinking a zero average rate means nothing changed in between.</li>
</ul></div>
