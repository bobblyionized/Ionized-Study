<h2>5.3 Common and Natural Logarithmic Functions</h2>
<p>A logarithm answers the question: <b>what exponent do I need?</b> $\log 1000=3$ because $10^3=1000$. Logarithms undo exponential functions, which is exactly what you need to solve for an unknown exponent.</p>

<h3>1. Common logarithms (base 10)</h3>
<div class="box"><h4>Definition</h4>For $v\gt0$, $\;\log v=u\;$ means $\;10^u=v$. So $\log v$ is the exponent you put on $10$ to get $v$.</div>
<p>$f(x)=\log x$ is the <b>inverse function</b> of $g(x)=10^x$: since $10^x$ passes the horizontal line test, it has an inverse, and swapping inputs and outputs of $10^x$ gives $\log x$.</p>
<ul><li>$\log1000=3$, $\log10=1$, $\log1=0$, $\log0.01=-2$ (since $10^{-2}=0.01$), $\log\sqrt{10}=\frac12$.</li>
<li>$\log v$ only exists for $v\gt0$: no power of $10$ is zero or negative. So $\log0$ and $\log(-5)$ are not defined.</li></ul>
<p><b>Translating.</b> $\log29\approx1.4624$ says the same thing as $10^{1.4624}\approx29$. Going the other way, $10^{-1.5}\approx0.0316$ says $\log0.0316\approx-1.5$.</p>

<h3>2. Natural logarithms (base $e$)</h3>
<div class="box"><h4>Definition</h4>For $v\gt0$, $\;\ln v=u\;$ means $\;e^u=v$. $\ln x$ is the inverse function of $e^x$.</div>
<p>$\ln e=1$, $\ln1=0$, $\ln e^5=5$, $\ln\frac1e=-1$. And $e^{3.4}\approx29.96$ says the same thing as $\ln29.96\approx3.4$.</p>

<h3>3. Properties</h3>
<div class="box"><h4>Inverse properties</h4>
<ul><li>$\log10^u=u$ for every $u$, and $10^{\log v}=v$ for every $v\gt0$.</li>
<li>$\ln e^u=u$ for every $u$, and $e^{\ln v}=v$ for every $v\gt0$.</li>
<li>$\log1=\ln1=0$; $\log10=\ln e=1$.</li></ul></div>
<p>These hold because each function undoes the other: $\ln e^u$ asks "what power of $e$ gives $e^u$?", and the answer is $u$. Examples: $\ln e^{x^2+1}=x^2+1$; $10^{\log7}=7$; $10^{\log7+2}=10^{\log7}\cdot10^2=700$.</p>
<p><b>Solving simple equations:</b> $\ln x=2$ means $x=e^2\approx7.389$. And $10^x=29$ means $x=\log29\approx1.4624$.</p>

<h3>4. Graphs</h3>
<p>The graph of an inverse function is the reflection across $y=x$. So the graph of $\ln x$ is the graph of $e^x$ reflected across that line.</p>
<div class="lfigs">
<div class="lplot" data-f="exp(x);log(x)|0.01|6;x" data-x="-3,6" data-y="-3,6" data-w="200" data-cap="$e^x$, $\ln x$, and the mirror line $y=x$"></div>
<div class="lplot" data-f="log10(x)|0.01|12;log(x)|0.01|12" data-x="-1,12" data-y="-3,3" data-w="200" data-cap="$\log x$ (lower) and $\ln x$ (upper)"></div>
</div>
<div class="box"><h4>Properties of $y=\log x$ and $y=\ln x$</h4><ul>
<li>Domain: $x\gt0$. Range: all real numbers.</li>
<li>$x$-intercept $(1,0)$. Also $(10,1)$ on $\log x$ and $(e,1)$ on $\ln x$.</li>
<li>Vertical asymptote $x=0$ (the $y$-axis): as $x\to0^+$, the graph drops to $-\infty$.</li>
<li>Increasing, but very slowly: $\log x$ only reaches $6$ at $x=1{,}000{,}000$.</li>
<li>Negative for $0\lt x\lt1$, positive for $x\gt1$.</li></ul></div>
<p><b>Domains of related functions:</b> the input of the log must be positive. $\ln(x+2)$ needs $x+2\gt0$, so $x\gt-2$; $\log(x^2-4)$ needs $x^2\gt4$, so $x\lt-2$ or $x\gt2$.</p>
<p><b>Transformations:</b> $y=\ln(x-3)+1$ is $\ln x$ shifted right $3$ and up $1$. Its vertical asymptote moves to $x=3$, and its domain is $x\gt3$.</p>

<div class="warn"><h4>Common traps</h4><ul>
<li>$\log v$ is an exponent, not a product: $\log1000\ne\frac{1000}{10}$.</li>
<li>Logs of $0$ or negative numbers don't exist.</li>
<li>$\ln x$ and $\log x$ have different bases: $\ln100\approx4.605$, while $\log100=2$.</li></ul></div>
