<h2>5.2 Exponential Functions</h2>
<p>An <b>exponential function</b> has the variable in the exponent: $f(x)=a^x$ with a fixed base $a\gt0$, $a\ne1$. Compare $x^2$ (variable base) with $2^x$ (variable exponent). Exponential functions model anything that grows or shrinks by the <b>same percentage</b> each time period.</p>

<h3>1. Graphs of $a^x$</h3>
<div class="lfigs">
<div class="lplot" data-f="pow(2,x);pow(3,x)" data-x="-4,4" data-y="-1,8" data-w="180" data-cap="$a\gt1$: $2^x$ and $3^x$ rise"></div>
<div class="lplot" data-f="pow(0.5,x);pow(1/3,x)" data-x="-4,4" data-y="-1,8" data-w="180" data-cap="$0\lt a\lt1$: $(\frac12)^x$ and $(\frac13)^x$ fall"></div>
</div>
<div class="box"><h4>Properties of $f(x)=a^x$</h4><ul>
<li>Domain: all real numbers. Range: all <b>positive</b> numbers ($a^x\gt0$ always).</li>
<li>$y$-intercept $(0,1)$, since $a^0=1$. The graph also passes through $(1,a)$.</li>
<li>Horizontal asymptote $y=0$ (the $x$-axis): on the left if $a\gt1$, on the right if $0\lt a\lt1$.</li>
<li>$a\gt1$: increasing, and it grows faster than any polynomial eventually. $0\lt a\lt1$: decreasing.</li></ul></div>
<p><b>Negative exponents flip the graph:</b> $3^{-x}=\left(3^{-1}\right)^x=\left(\frac13\right)^x$, the reflection of $3^x$ across the $y$-axis. In $a^{kx}$, the size of $k$ controls the steepness; its sign controls rising or falling.</p>
<p><b>How fast is "exponential"?</b> $2^{50}\approx1.13\times10^{15}$. On a graph with $1$-inch units, the height at $x=50$ would reach past the sun.</p>
<p><b>Transformations</b> work as in Section 3.4: $g(x)=2^{x-3}+1$ is $2^x$ shifted right $3$ and up $1$, so its asymptote is $y=1$ and its range is $y\gt1$.</p>

<h3>2. Exponential growth</h3>
<div class="box"><h4>Growth model</h4>A quantity that starts at $P$ and grows by $r$ (as a decimal) per time period is $f(x)=P(1+r)^x=Pa^x$ after $x$ periods. The <b>growth factor</b> $a=1+r$ is greater than $1$.</div>
<p><b>Why:</b> each period multiplies by $1+r$: $P\to P(1+r)\to P(1+r)^2\to\cdots$. Example: $\$5000$ at $3\%$ compounded annually for $9$ years: $5000(1.03)^9\approx\$6523.87$.</p>
<p><b>Finding the factor from data.</b> A culture has $1000$ bacteria, and $7600$ five hours later. Then $1000a^5=7600$, so $a^5=7.6$ and $a=7.6^{1/5}$. After $24$ hours: $1000\cdot7.6^{24/5}\approx16{,}900{,}721$ bacteria.</p>

<h3>3. Exponential decay and half-life</h3>
<p>If a fixed percentage is <i>lost</i> each period, $f(x)=P(1-r)^x$ with factor $0\lt a\lt1$. Example: a filter removes $30\%$ of impurities, so $70\%$ remain after each layer: $f(x)=(0.7)^x$ of the original remains after $x$ layers.</p>
<div class="box"><h4>Radioactive decay</h4>The <b>half-life</b> $h$ is the time for half of a substance to decay. Starting from $c$, the amount left after $x$ years is $M(x)=c\,(0.5)^{x/h}$.</div>
<p>Each time $x$ increases by $h$, the exponent $\frac xh$ goes up by $1$ and the amount halves. Plutonium-239 has $h=24{,}360$ years, so $M(x)=(0.5)^{x/24360}\approx0.99997^x$: a base so close to $1$ that it decays extremely slowly.</p>
<div class="lfigs"><div class="lplot" data-f="80*pow(0.5,x/5)" data-x="0,25" data-y="0,90" data-w="230" data-cap="$80(0.5)^{x/5}$: half-life $5$ (80, 40, 20, 10, ...)"></div></div>

<h3>4. The natural exponential function</h3>
<p>The number $e\approx2.718281828459$ is irrational (Section 5.2.A shows where it comes from). The <b>natural exponential function</b> is $f(x)=e^x$, which sits between $2^x$ and $3^x$. Models are often written $f(t)=Pe^{kt}$: growth if $k\gt0$, decay if $k\lt0$.</p>
<p>Example: if $P(t)=227e^{0.0093t}$ million people ($t=0$ in 1980), then in 2000, $P(20)=227e^{0.186}\approx273.4$ million.</p>

<h3>5. Other exponential functions</h3>
<ul><li><b>Logistic (inhibited) growth:</b> $p(t)=\dfrac{c}{1+ae^{-kt}}$ grows quickly at first, then levels off at the <b>upper limit</b> $c$ (as $t$ grows, $e^{-kt}\to0$). For $p(t)=\dfrac{20000}{1+24e^{-t/4}}$ the limit is $20{,}000$ and the starting value is $p(0)=\frac{20000}{25}=800$.</li>
<li>$f(x)=\dfrac{e^x+e^{-x}}2$ is an <b>even</b> function (replacing $x$ by $-x$ swaps the two terms), and its graph is a U-shaped curve called a catenary.</li></ul>
<div class="lfigs"><div class="lplot" data-f="20000/(1+24*exp(-x/4))" data-x="0,40" data-y="0,22000" data-w="240" data-cap="Logistic growth levels off at $20{,}000$"></div></div>

<div class="warn"><h4>Common traps</h4><ul>
<li>Growth by $r$ per period means multiplying by $1+r$ (not $r$): $3\%$ growth is $\times1.03$.</li>
<li>$a^x$ is never $0$ or negative, so its range is $(0,\infty)$, not all reals.</li>
<li>For half-life, the exponent is $\frac xh$ (time divided by half-life), not $x\cdot h$.</li></ul></div>
