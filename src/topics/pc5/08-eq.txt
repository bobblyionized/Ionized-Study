<h2>5.5 Algebraic Solutions of Exponential and Logarithmic Equations</h2>
<p>Two tools do almost all the work: <b>take a logarithm</b> to bring an unknown exponent down, and <b>exponentiate</b> to undo a logarithm.</p>

<h3>1. Exponential equations</h3>
<div class="box"><h4>Method</h4><ol><li>Isolate the exponential expression.</li><li>Take $\ln$ (or $\log$) of both sides.</li><li>Use the Power Law to bring the exponent down, then solve the resulting linear equation.</li></ol></div>
<ul><li>$5^x=2$: $\;\ln5^x=\ln2$, so $x\ln5=\ln2$ and $x=\dfrac{\ln2}{\ln5}\approx0.4307$.</li>
<li>$e^{3x}=7$: $\;3x=\ln7$, so $x=\frac{\ln7}3\approx0.6486$ (with base $e$, $\ln$ undoes it directly).</li>
<li>$10^{x+1}=500$: $\;x+1=\log500$, so $x=\log500-1=\log50\approx1.699$.</li>
<li>Variables on both sides: $2^{4x-1}=3^{1-x}$. Take $\ln$: $(4x-1)\ln2=(1-x)\ln3$. Collect the $x$ terms: $x(4\ln2+\ln3)=\ln3+\ln2$, so $x=\dfrac{\ln6}{\ln48}\approx0.4628$.</li></ul>
<p><b>When the bases match</b>, you don't need logs: $2^{x+3}=2^{5}$ gives $x+3=5$, because $2^x$ is one-to-one.</p>

<h3>2. Equations quadratic in $e^x$</h3>
<p>$e^x-5e^{-x}=4$: multiply by $e^x$ (never zero) to get $(e^x)^2-4e^x-5=0$. With $u=e^x$: $(u-5)(u+1)=0$. Since $e^x\gt0$, only $u=5$ works: $x=\ln5$.</p>

<h3>3. Applications</h3>
<ul><li><b>Half-life from data.</b> $M(x)=c(0.5)^{x/h}$. If $20$ mg of strontium-90 becomes $6.071$ mg after $43$ years: $(0.5)^{43/h}=0.30355$. Take $\ln$: $\frac{43}h\ln0.5=\ln0.30355$, so $h=\dfrac{43\ln0.5}{\ln0.30355}\approx25$ years.</li>
<li><b>Carbon-14 dating.</b> Carbon-14 has half-life $5730$ years. If a fossil has $20\%$ of its original carbon-14: $(0.5)^{t/5730}=0.2$, so $t=\dfrac{5730\ln0.2}{\ln0.5}\approx13{,}305$ years.</li>
<li><b>Growth rate.</b> If $P(t)=P_0e^{kt}$ grows from $10$ to $15$ million in $8$ years: $e^{8k}=1.5$, so $k=\frac{\ln1.5}8\approx0.0507$ ($5.07\%$ continuous growth).</li>
<li><b>Doubling time</b> at continuous rate $r$: $e^{rt}=2$, so $t=\frac{\ln2}{r}$. At $5\%$: $\frac{0.6931}{0.05}\approx13.9$ years.</li></ul>

<h3>4. Logarithmic equations</h3>
<div class="box"><h4>Method</h4><ol><li>Use the laws to combine into a single logarithm on one side.</li><li>Rewrite in exponential form (exponentiate).</li><li>Solve, then <b>check</b>: a solution that makes any original log's input zero or negative is extraneous.</li></ol></div>
<ul><li>$\ln(x+1)-\ln x=1$: $\;\ln\frac{x+1}x=1$, so $\frac{x+1}x=e$, $x+1=ex$, and $x=\dfrac1{e-1}\approx0.582$ ✓.</li>
<li>$\log(x-16)=2-\log(x-1)$: $\;\log\big[(x-16)(x-1)\big]=2$, so $(x-16)(x-1)=100$, i.e. $x^2-17x-84=0=(x-21)(x+4)$. $x=-4$ makes $x-16$ negative, so it's extraneous. Answer: $x=21$.</li>
<li>$\log_2x+\log_2(x-2)=3$: $\;x(x-2)=8$, so $x=4$ or $x=-2$. Only $x=4$ checks.</li></ul>

<div class="warn"><h4>Common traps</h4><ul>
<li>$\ln(x+1)\ne\ln x+\ln1$. Combine logs only with the Product, Quotient, and Power Laws.</li>
<li>$\dfrac{\ln2}{\ln5}\ne\ln\dfrac25$. Leave it as a quotient (or compute it).</li>
<li>Skipping the check: the combined equation can have solutions the original can't.</li></ul></div>
