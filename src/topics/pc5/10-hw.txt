<h3>Tue 10/27 · Lessons 5.3–5.4, 5.4A <span class="pill no">HW Quiz</span></h3>
<p>Book pages: <button class="chip" data-bk-open="pc5:book:43">p. 383 (5.3)</button> <button class="chip" data-bk-open="pc5:book:50">p. 390 (5.4)</button> <button class="chip" data-bk-open="pc5:book:57">p. 397 (5.4A)</button> · Lessons: <a href="#/s/pc5-log/lesson">5.3</a>, <a href="#/s/pc5-laws/lesson">5.4</a>, <a href="#/s/pc5-base/lesson">5.4.A</a></p>
<div class="box"><h4>What you need for the quiz</h4><ul>
<li><b>Inverses undo each other.</b> $\log 10^{k}=k$, $10^{\log x}=x$, $\ln e^{k}=k$, $e^{\ln x}=x$ (for $x\gt0$). Same for any base: $\log_b b^{k}=k$ and $b^{\log_b x}=x$.</li>
<li><b>Translate.</b> $\log_b x=y$ means $b^{y}=x$. So $\log_{16}4=\frac12$ because $16^{1/2}=4$.</li>
<li><b>Domain.</b> You can only take the log of a positive number: $\ln(x+1)$ needs $x+1\gt0$, so $x\gt-1$.</li>
<li><b>Laws</b> (any base): $\log(vw)=\log v+\log w$, $\log\frac vw=\log v-\log w$, $\log v^{k}=k\log v$. A plain number like $1$ becomes a log first: $1=\log 10=\log_2 2=\ln e$.</li>
<li><b>Not laws:</b> $\log(a+b)\ne\log a+\log b$, $\log_b(5x)\ne5\log_b x$, $(\ln x)^3\ne\ln x^3$.</li>
<li><b>Graph shifts.</b> $\log(x-3)$ is $\log x$ moved right 3: vertical asymptote $x=3$, $x$-intercept $(4,0)$.</li>
<li><b>Average rate of change</b> from $a$ to $b$: $\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}$.</li></ul></div>
<div class="tip"><b>Writing as one log, step by step:</b> move every coefficient inside as a power first ($2\ln(x+1)=\ln(x+1)^2$), then combine: pluses multiply, minuses divide. Factor at the end ($\frac{x^2-9}{x+3}=x-3$).</div>

<h3>Thu 10/29 · Lesson 5.5: Using Logs to Solve Equations</h3>
<p>Book pages: <button class="chip" data-bk-open="pc5:book:66">p. 406 (5.5)</button> <button class="chip" data-bk-open="pc5:book:34">p. 374 (5.2A)</button> · Lesson: <a href="#/s/pc5-eq/lesson">5.5</a></p>
<div class="box"><h4>Three ways to solve</h4><ul>
<li><b>Same base:</b> rewrite both sides with one base, then set exponents equal. $3^{x+1}=9^{5x}=3^{10x}$, so $x+1=10x$.</li>
<li><b>Take a log of both sides:</b> $3^x=5\Rightarrow x\ln3=\ln5\Rightarrow x=\dfrac{\ln5}{\ln3}$.</li>
<li><b>Quadratic in disguise:</b> $e^{2x}-5e^x+6=0$ with $u=e^x$ is $u^2-5u+6=0$. Throw out any $u\le0$, since $e^x$ and $4^x$ are always positive.</li></ul></div>
<div class="warn"><b>Log equations:</b> combine into one log on each side, drop the logs, solve, then <b>check every answer in the original</b>. Any value that makes the inside of a log zero or negative is extraneous.</div>
<p><b>Half-life:</b> $M(x)=c\,(.5)^{x/h}$, where $h$ is the half-life. <b>Interest:</b> $A=P(1+r)^t$ per period, and $A=Pe^{rt}$ for continuous compounding.</p>

<p>The two worksheets are given out in class. The last problems in the Problem Set are practice in the same style (exponential growth and decay, then a logs/ln review), so you can warm up or check yourself.</p>
<div class="box"><h4>Growth and decay models</h4><ul>
<li>Grows by $r$ per period: $y=a(1+r)^t$. Shrinks by $r$: $y=a(1-r)^t$.</li>
<li>Continuous: $y=ae^{kt}$ ($k\gt0$ growth, $k\lt0$ decay).</li>
<li>Doubling every $d$: $y=a\cdot2^{t/d}$. Half-life $h$: $y=a(.5)^{t/h}$.</li></ul></div>
