<h3>5.1 &amp; 5.1.A Radicals, rational exponents, radical equations</h3>
<p>Lessons: <a href="#/s/pc5-rad/lesson">5.1</a>, <a href="#/s/pc5-radeq/lesson">5.1.A</a></p>
<div class="box"><h4>Key facts</h4><ul>
<li>$a^{m/n}=\sqrt[n]{a^m}=\left(\sqrt[n]{a}\right)^m$ and $a^{-r}=\dfrac1{a^r}$.</li>
<li>Exponent laws: $a^ra^s=a^{r+s}$, $\dfrac{a^r}{a^s}=a^{r-s}$, $(a^r)^s=a^{rs}$, $(ab)^r=a^rb^r$.</li>
<li>Difference of squares works with fractional powers too: $(u+v)(u-v)=u^2-v^2$, so $(x^{1/3}+y^{1/3})(x^{1/3}-y^{1/3})=x^{2/3}-y^{2/3}$.</li>
<li><b>Quadratic in disguise:</b> $x^{2/3}-3x^{1/3}-18=0$ is $u^2-3u-18=0$ with $u=x^{1/3}$.</li>
<li><b>Radical equations:</b> isolate a radical, raise both sides to the same power, solve, then <b>check every answer</b> in the original. Squaring can create extraneous solutions.</li>
<li>A square root is never negative, so sometimes you can see there is no solution before doing any algebra.</li></ul></div>

<h3>5.2 Exponential functions</h3>
<p>Lesson: <a href="#/s/pc5-exp/lesson">5.2</a></p>
<div class="box"><h4>Key facts</h4><ul>
<li>$f(x)=b^x$ passes through $(0,1)$ and has the horizontal asymptote $y=0$. It increases if $b\gt1$ and decreases if $0\lt b\lt1$.</li>
<li>Shifts move the asymptote: $2^x-1$ is $2^x$ moved down 1, so the asymptote is $y=-1$ and it passes through $(0,0)$.</li>
<li>Growth: $P(1+r)^x$ or $Pe^{kx}$ with $k\gt0$. Decay: $P(1-r)^x$ or $Pe^{kx}$ with $k\lt0$.</li></ul></div>

<h3>5.2.A Compound interest</h3>
<p>Lesson: <a href="#/s/pc5-int/lesson">5.2.A</a></p>
<div class="box"><h4>Key facts</h4><ul>
<li>$A=P(1+r)^t$, where $r$ is the rate <b>per period</b> and $t$ is the <b>number of periods</b>. Quarterly at $4.5\%$ for 6 years: $r=\frac{.045}4$, $t=24$.</li>
<li>Continuous compounding: $A=Pe^{rt}$.</li>
<li><b>Present value</b> (how much to invest now): solve for $P=\dfrac{A}{(1+r)^t}$.</li></ul></div>

<h3>5.3, 5.4, 5.4.A Logarithms and their properties</h3>
<p>Lessons: <a href="#/s/pc5-log/lesson">5.3</a>, <a href="#/s/pc5-laws/lesson">5.4</a>, <a href="#/s/pc5-base/lesson">5.4.A</a></p>
<div class="box"><h4>Key facts</h4><ul>
<li><b>Translate:</b> $\ln a=c$ means $e^c=a$; $\log a=c$ means $10^c=a$; $\log_b a=c$ means $b^c=a$.</li>
<li><b>Inverses:</b> $\ln e^k=k$, $e^{\ln x}=x$, $\log 10^k=k$, $10^{\log x}=x$.</li>
<li><b>Laws:</b> $\ln(vw)=\ln v+\ln w$, $\ln\frac vw=\ln v-\ln w$, $\ln v^k=k\ln v$ (same for any base). Move coefficients in as exponents first, then combine.</li>
<li><b>Not laws:</b> $\ln(a+b)\ne\ln a+\ln b$ and $\ln 10\ne(\ln2)(\ln5)$.</li>
<li><b>Domain:</b> you can only take the log of a positive number. $\log(-.001)$ is not a real number.</li>
<li><b>Graphs:</b> $\ln x$ has the vertical asymptote $x=0$ and passes through $(1,0)$. $\ln(x+4)-2$ moves it left 4 and down 2.</li>
<li>Change of base: $\log_b v=\dfrac{\ln v}{\ln b}$.</li></ul></div>

<h3>5.5 Solving equations using logs</h3>
<p>Lesson: <a href="#/s/pc5-eq/lesson">5.5</a></p>
<div class="box"><h4>Key facts</h4><ul>
<li><b>Same base:</b> rewrite both sides with one base and set the exponents equal ($9=3^2$).</li>
<li><b>Log equations:</b> combine into one log on each side, drop the logs, solve, then check. Any answer that puts a zero or negative number inside a log is extraneous.</li>
<li>$\log A=2+\log B$ means $\log\frac AB=2$, so $\frac AB=100$.</li>
<li><b>Half-life $h$:</b> $M(t)=c\,(.5)^{t/h}$.</li></ul></div>

Q: <b>Book #5.</b> Simplify: $(x^{1/3}+y^{1/3})(x^{1/3}-y^{1/3})$
L: 1
A: x^{2/3}-y^{2/3}
W: x^{1/9}-y^{1/9}
W: x^{2/3}+y^{2/3}
W: x^{2/3}-2x^{1/3}y^{1/3}+y^{2/3}
W: x-y
S: This is a difference of squares, $(u+v)(u-v)=u^2-v^2$, with $u=x^{1/3}$ and $v=y^{1/3}$.
$u^2=(x^{1/3})^2=x^{2/3}$ and $v^2=y^{2/3}$, so the product is $x^{2/3}-y^{2/3}$.
(The middle terms $-x^{1/3}y^{1/3}+x^{1/3}y^{1/3}$ cancel.)

Q: <b>Book #6.</b> Simplify: $2a^{3/5}\left(5a^{7/5}-4a^{-3/5}\right)$
L: 2
A: 10a^2-8
W: 10a^2-8a^{-9/25}
W: 10a^{21/25}-8
W: 7a^2-6
W: 10a^2-8a^{6/5}
S: Distribute, then add exponents when multiplying powers of $a$.
$2a^{3/5}\cdot5a^{7/5}=10a^{3/5+7/5}=10a^{10/5}=10a^2$.
$2a^{3/5}\cdot4a^{-3/5}=8a^{3/5-3/5}=8a^0=8$.
So the answer is $10a^2-8$.

Q: <b>Book #13.</b> Find all real solutions: $3\sqrt{x+3}+\sqrt{x-1}=2$
L: 2
A: \text{No solutions}
W: x=1
W: x=-3
W: x=\dfrac{13}{9}
W: x=1\text{ or }x=-3
S: Both square roots need nonnegative insides, so $x\ge1$.
For $x\ge1$, $\sqrt{x+3}\ge\sqrt4=2$, so $3\sqrt{x+3}\ge6$, and $\sqrt{x-1}\ge0$.
The left side is always at least $6$, so it can never equal $2$. There are no solutions.

Q: <b>Book #14.</b> Find all real solutions: $x^{2/3}-3x^{1/3}-18=0$
L: 2
A: x=-27\text{ or }x=216
W: x=6\text{ or }x=-3
W: x=36\text{ or }x=9
W: x=216\text{ only}
W: x=27\text{ or }x=-216
S: Let $u=x^{1/3}$. Then $x^{2/3}=u^2$ and the equation is $u^2-3u-18=0$.
Factor: $(u-6)(u+3)=0$, so $u=6$ or $u=-3$.
Undo the substitution by cubing: $x^{1/3}=6\Rightarrow x=216$; $x^{1/3}=-3\Rightarrow x=-27$. Cube roots of negatives are fine, so both work.

Q: <b>Book #19.</b> Which describes the graph of $g(x)=2^x-1$?
L: 1
A: Increasing, horizontal asymptote $y=-1$, passes through $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$
W: Increasing, horizontal asymptote $y=0$, passes through $(0,1)$
W: Increasing, horizontal asymptote $y=1$, passes through $(0,2)$
W: Decreasing, horizontal asymptote $y=-1$, passes through $(0,0)$
W: Increasing, vertical asymptote $x=-1$, passes through $(0,0)$
S: $2^x$ passes through $(0,1)$ with horizontal asymptote $y=0$. Subtracting 1 moves everything down 1.
So $2^x-1$ has asymptote $y=-1$ and passes through $(0,0)$; also $g(1)=2-1=1$. It still increases, since the base $2\gt1$.

Q: <b>Book #21.</b> Which describes the graph of $h(x)=\ln(x+4)-2$?
L: 2
A: Vertical asymptote $x=-4$, passes through $(-3,-2)$, crosses the $x$-axis at $x=e^2-4\approx3.39$
W: Vertical asymptote $x=4$, passes through $(5,-2)$, crosses the $x$-axis at $x=e^2+4$
W: Vertical asymptote $x=-4$, passes through $(-3,0)$, crosses the $x$-axis at $x=-3$
W: Horizontal asymptote $y=-2$, passes through $(0,\ln4)$
W: Vertical asymptote $x=-2$, passes through $(-1,-4)$, crosses the $x$-axis at $x=e^4-2$
S: $\ln x$ has vertical asymptote $x=0$ and passes through $(1,0)$. $\ln(x+4)$ moves it <b>left</b> 4, and $-2$ moves it down 2.
So the asymptote is $x=-4$ and the point $(1,0)$ moves to $(-3,-2)$.
$x$-intercept: $\ln(x+4)=2\Rightarrow x+4=e^2\Rightarrow x=e^2-4\approx3.39$.

Q: <b>Book #49.</b> Which statement is true?
L: 2
A: \ln\tfrac17+\ln7=0
W: \ln10=(\ln2)(\ln5)
W: \ln\tfrac e6=\ln e+\ln6
W: \ln(-e)=-1
W: \text{None of these is true}

Q: <b>Book #55.</b> Solve for $x$: $9^{9-x}=3^{x^2-5x}$
L: 2
A: x=-3\text{ or }x=6
W: x=3\text{ or }x=-6
W: x=9
W: x=-3\text{ only}
W: x=2\text{ or }x=9
S: Make the bases match: $9^{9-x}=(3^2)^{9-x}=3^{18-2x}$.
Set the exponents equal: $18-2x=x^2-5x$, so $x^2-3x-18=0$.
Factor: $(x-6)(x+3)=0$, so $x=6$ or $x=-3$. Both work, since any real exponent is allowed.

Q: <b>Book #61.</b> Solve for $x$: $\ln5x+\ln(2x-4)=\ln30$
L: 2
A: x=3
W: x=3\text{ or }x=-1
W: x=-1
W: x=\dfrac{17}{7}
W: x=5
S: Product Law: $\ln\big(5x(2x-4)\big)=\ln30$, so $10x^2-20x=30$.
Divide by 10: $x^2-2x-3=0$, which factors as $(x-3)(x+1)=0$, so $x=3$ or $x=-1$.
Check: $x=-1$ makes $\ln5x=\ln(-5)$, which is undefined, so it is extraneous. Only $x=3$ works.

Q: <b>Book #63.</b> Solve for $x$: $\log(4x^2-9)=2+\log(2x-3)$
L: 3
A: x=48.5
W: x=50
W: x=48.5\text{ or }x=-1.5
W: x=98.5
W: \text{No solutions}
S: Move the log over: $\log(4x^2-9)-\log(2x-3)=2$, so $\log\dfrac{4x^2-9}{2x-3}=2$.
Factor: $\dfrac{(2x-3)(2x+3)}{2x-3}=2x+3$. So $\log(2x+3)=2$, which gives $2x+3=10^2=100$.
$2x=97$, so $x=48.5$. Check: $2x-3=94\gt0$ and $4x^2-9\gt0$, so it works.

Q: <b>Book #69.</b> How much should be invested at $4.5\%$ per year, compounded quarterly, to have $\$5000$ in 6 years?
L: 3
A: \approx\$3822.66
W: \approx\$3839.48
W: \approx\$3820.00
W: \approx\$4775.00
W: \approx\$6539.96
S: Quarterly: the rate per period is $\frac{.045}4=.01125$, and there are $6\cdot4=24$ periods.
$5000=P(1.01125)^{24}$, so $P=\dfrac{5000}{(1.01125)^{24}}\approx\dfrac{5000}{1.30799}\approx\$3822.66$.
($\$3839.48$ comes from compounding yearly, $\frac{5000}{1.045^6}$; $\$6539.96$ is what $\$5000$ would <i>grow</i> to.)
