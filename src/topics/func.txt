<script type="text/plain" id="L-func-eval">
<h2>Evaluation, Domain &amp; Range</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Key ideas</h4><ul>
<li><b>Evaluate carefully:</b> substitute with parentheses, e.g. $f(-2)$ for $f(x)=2x^2-3x+1$ is $2(4)+6+1$.</li>
<li><b>Custom operations</b> ($a\star b$, $a\nabla b$) are just functions of two variables. Work from the innermost parentheses outward, and remember they are usually <i>not</i> commutative or associative.</li>
<li><b>Domain:</b> denominators $\ne0$, even roots need radicand $\ge0$, logs need argument $>0$.</li>
<li><b>Range:</b> find the extreme values (complete the square, or bound a fraction like $\frac{6}{x^2+2}\in(0,3]$).</li>
<li>A linear function is determined by two values: slope $=\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}$.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Odd-function trick</h3>
<p>For $f(x)=ax^7+bx^3+cx-5$, the part $g(x)=f(x)+5$ is odd. So $f(-7)=7$ means $g(-7)=12$, $g(7)=-12$, and $f(7)=-17$. You never need $a,b,c$.</p>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example.</b> Find the minimum of $f(f(x))$ for $f(x)=x^2+6x+7$.
<details><summary>Solution</summary>$f(x)=(x+3)^2-2\ge-2$. On $[-2,\infty)$, $f$ is increasing, so the minimum of $f(y)$ with $y\ge-2$ is $f(-2)=-1$.</details></div>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-func-comp">
<h2>Composition &amp; Inverses</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Key ideas</h4><ul>
<li>$(f\circ g)(x)=f(g(x))$: apply $g$ first. In general $f(g(x))\ne g(f(x))$.</li>
<li><b>Inverse:</b> swap $x$ and $y$ and solve for $y$. Shortcut: $f^{-1}(a)=b$ exactly when $f(b)=a$, so to find $f^{-1}(5)$ solve $f(x)=5$.</li>
<li>For $f(x)=\frac{ax+b}{cx+d}$: $f^{-1}(x)=\frac{dx-b}{-cx+a}$.</li>
<li><b>Recovering $f$:</b> if $f(2x-1)=6x+5$, set $t=2x-1$, so $x=\frac{t+1}2$ and $f(t)=3t+8$. Or just pick the $x$ that makes the input what you need.</li>
<li><b>Iteration:</b> compute $f(x), f(f(x)), \dots$ and look for a cycle. $\frac{1}{1-x}$ has period 3; $\frac{x+1}{x-1}$ is its own inverse (period 2).</li>
<li>Iterating $f(x)=ax+b$: $f^{(n)}(x)=a^nx+b\frac{a^n-1}{a-1}$.</li>
</ul></div>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-func-trans">
<h2>Transformations &amp; Graphs</h2>
<table class="t"><tr><th>New graph</th><th>Effect</th></tr>
<tr><td>$y=f(x-h)+k$</td><td>shift right $h$, up $k$</td></tr>
<tr><td>$y=-f(x)$</td><td>reflect over the $x$-axis</td></tr>
<tr><td>$y=f(-x)$</td><td>reflect over the $y$-axis</td></tr>
<tr><td>$y=af(x)$</td><td>vertical stretch by $a$</td></tr>
<tr><td>$y=f(bx)$</td><td>horizontal compression by $b$ (zeros divide by $b$)</td></tr>
<tr><td>$y=|f(x)|$</td><td>reflect the parts below the axis upward</td></tr></table>
<h3>Even and odd</h3>
<p>Even: $f(-x)=f(x)$ (symmetric about the $y$-axis). Odd: $f(-x)=-f(x)$ (symmetric about the origin, and $f(0)=0$ if defined).</p>
<h3>Absolute value graphs</h3>
<p>$y=a-|x-h|$ is a tent with peak $(h,a)$ and slopes $\pm1$. It meets the $x$-axis at $h\pm a$, so it encloses a triangle of area $a^2$.</p>
<h3>Counting solutions</h3>
<p>For $|g(x)|=c$, solve $g(x)=c$ and $g(x)=-c$ separately. For nested absolute values, peel one layer at a time.</p>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-func-floor">
<h2>Piecewise, Floor &amp; Ceiling</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Definitions</h4><ul>
<li>$\lfloor x\rfloor$ is the greatest integer $\le x$; $\lceil x\rceil$ is the least integer $\ge x$; $\{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor\in[0,1)$.</li>
<li>$\lfloor x\rfloor=n \iff n\le x\lt n+1$. Turn floor equations into inequalities.</li>
<li>$\lfloor-x\rfloor=-\lceil x\rceil$, and $\lfloor x\rfloor+\lfloor-x\rfloor=-1$ for non-integers.</li>
<li>$\lfloor\frac{n}{k}\rfloor$ is the number of multiples of $k$ in $\{1,\dots,n\}$.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Solving $x\lfloor x\rfloor=N$</h3>
<p>Let $n=\lfloor x\rfloor$, so $x=\frac Nn$, and require $n\le\frac Nn\lt n+1$, i.e. $n^2\le N\lt n^2+n$.</p>
<h3>Sums of floors</h3>
<p>Group equal values. $\lfloor\frac k3\rfloor=m$ for exactly three consecutive $k$, so $\sum_{k=1}^{100}\lfloor k/3\rfloor$ is $3(1+\dots+32)+2\cdot33$.</p>
<h3>Piecewise functions</h3>
<p>Evaluate by first checking which rule applies. For compositions, find the inner value first, then pick the rule for <i>that</i> value.</p>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-func-feq">
<h2>Functional Equations</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Toolkit</h4><ul>
<li><b>Plug in special values:</b> $x=0$, $y=0$, $x=y$, $y=1$, $y=-x$.</li>
<li><b>Pair up substitutions:</b> for $f(x)+2f\!\left(\frac1x\right)=3x$, also write it at $\frac1x$, then solve the $2\times2$ system for $f(x)$. Likewise $x\to-x$ or $x\to1-x$.</li>
<li><b>Additive</b> $f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y)$: $f(n)=nf(1)$ for integers (and rationals).</li>
<li><b>Log-like</b> $f(xy)=f(x)+f(y)$: $f(2^a3^b)=af(2)+bf(3)$.</li>
<li><b>Shift relations:</b> $f(x+2)=-f(x)$ means $f(x+4)=f(x)$, i.e. period 4.</li>
<li><b>Pairing sums:</b> if $f(x)+f(1-x)=1$, sums over $\frac kn$ pair up neatly.</li>
</ul></div>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example.</b> $f(x)f(y)-f(xy)=x+y$ for all $x,y$. Find $f(2026)$.
<details><summary>Solution</summary>$x=y=0$: $f(0)^2=f(0)$. With $y=0$: $f(0)(f(x)-1)=x$, which forces $f(0)=1$ and $f(x)=x+1$. So $f(2026)=2027$.</details></div>
</script>
