<h2>Linear Equations &amp; Systems</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Core methods</h4><ul>
<li><b>Isolate:</b> move all $x$ terms to one side, constants to the other, divide.</li>
<li><b>Substitution:</b> solve one equation for a variable and plug it into the other.</li>
<li><b>Elimination:</b> scale equations so one variable cancels when you add or subtract.</li>
<li>A $2\times2$ system $a_1x+b_1y=c_1,\ a_2x+b_2y=c_2$ has <b>no solution</b> when $\frac{a_1}{a_2}=\frac{b_1}{b_2}\ne\frac{c_1}{c_2}$ (parallel lines) and <b>infinitely many</b> when all three ratios are equal.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>1. Answer the question that is asked</h3>
<p>AMC problems often ask for a combination like $a+b$ or $3x+2y$, not each variable. Look for a way to get it directly: adding $2a+3b=12$ and $3a+2b=13$ gives $5(a+b)=25$, so $a+b=5$ immediately.</p>
<h3>2. Symmetric systems: add everything</h3>
<p>For $x+y=10,\ y+z=14,\ z+x=18$, adding gives $2(x+y+z)=42$. Then each variable is the total minus one equation: $x=21-14=7$, etc. Cyclic systems like $x+2y+3z,\ 2x+3y+z,\ 3x+y+2z$ also collapse when added.</p>
<h3>3. Products instead of sums</h3>
<p>If $xy=24,\ xz=48,\ yz=72$, multiply all three: $(xyz)^2=24\cdot48\cdot72$. Then divide by each equation. Reciprocal systems ($\frac1x+\frac1y=5$) become linear with $u=\frac1x, v=\frac1y$.</p>
<h3>4. Translating word problems</h3>
<ul><li>Ages: "in $n$ years" adds $n$ to <i>every</i> person's age.</li>
<li>Coins: count equation and value equation (work in cents).</li>
<li>Digits: a two-digit number with digits $a,b$ is $10a+b$; reversed it is $10b+a$.</li></ul>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example.</b> Mary is 3 times as old as her son. In 12 years she will be twice as old. How old is Mary?
<details><summary>Solution</summary>Son $s$, Mary $3s$: $3s+12=2(s+12)\Rightarrow s=12$, so Mary is $36$.</details></div>
<div class="tip"><h4>AMC tip</h4>Before solving for every variable, add, subtract, or multiply the given equations and see if the requested expression appears.</div>
</script>

<h2>Ratios, Rates &amp; Percents</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Key ideas</h4><ul>
<li>A ratio $a:b=2:3$ means $a=2k,\ b=3k$ for some $k$. Introduce $k$.</li>
<li>Chaining ratios: from $a:b$ and $b:c$, rescale so the $b$ values match, then read off $a:c$.</li>
<li>Percent change uses a <b>multiplier</b>: $+20\%$ is $\times1.2$, $-25\%$ is $\times0.75$. Successive changes multiply.</li>
<li>Reverse percent: if the price after a $25\%$ discount is $36$, the original is $36/0.75=48$.</li>
<li>$x\%$ of $y$ equals $y\%$ of $x$.</li>
<li>Direct variation $y=kx$; inverse variation $xy=k$ (e.g. workers × time = constant job).</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Common traps</h3>
<ul><li>$+20\%$ then $-20\%$ is <b>not</b> zero change: $1.2\times0.8=0.96$, a $4\%$ loss.</li>
<li>"Percent of what?" A markup is a percent of cost; a discount is a percent of the marked price.</li>
<li>Two years of $10\%$ growth is $1.1^2=1.21$, i.e. $21\%$, not $20\%$.</li></ul>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example.</b> Boys : girls $=4:5$. After 6 boys leave and 6 girls join, the ratio is $2:3$. How many students were there originally?
<details><summary>Solution</summary>$\frac{4k-6}{5k+6}=\frac23\Rightarrow12k-18=10k+12\Rightarrow k=15$. Total $9k=135$.</details></div>
</script>

<h2>Work, Distance &amp; Mixtures</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Formulas</h4><ul>
<li>Distance $=$ rate × time.</li>
<li><b>Average speed</b> $=\dfrac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}}$. For two equal distances at speeds $a$ and $b$: $\dfrac{2ab}{a+b}$ (the harmonic mean, not $\frac{a+b}2$).</li>
<li>Moving toward each other: closing speed $=$ sum of speeds. Same direction: difference.</li>
<li>Work: if A finishes a job in $a$ hours, A's rate is $\frac1a$ job per hour. Rates add: together time $=\dfrac{1}{\frac1a+\frac1b}=\dfrac{ab}{a+b}$. A drain contributes a negative rate.</li>
<li>Mixtures: amount of pure substance $=$ concentration × volume. Set up one equation for the pure substance.</li>
<li>Currents and wind: downstream speed $b+c$, upstream $b-c$.</li>
</ul></div>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example.</b> How many liters of pure acid must be added to $20$ L of $30\%$ acid to make $50\%$ acid?
<details><summary>Solution</summary>$6+x=0.5(20+x)\Rightarrow x=8$ L.</details></div>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example.</b> Walking on a stopped escalator takes 90 s; standing on the moving escalator takes 60 s. How long does walking on the moving escalator take?
<details><summary>Solution</summary>Rates add: $\frac1{90}+\frac1{60}=\frac1{36}$, so 36 s.</details></div>
<div class="tip"><h4>AMC tip</h4>Write everything as a rate (per hour, per minute) and convert minutes to hours carefully: 5 minutes is $\frac1{12}$ hour.</div>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-alg-fact">
<h2>Factoring &amp; Special Products</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Identities to know cold</h4><ul>
<li>$a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b)$</li>
<li>$a^3-b^3=(a-b)(a^2+ab+b^2)$, $\quad a^3+b^3=(a+b)(a^2-ab+b^2)$</li>
<li>$(a\pm b)^2=a^2\pm2ab+b^2$, $\quad a^2+b^2=(a+b)^2-2ab$</li>
<li>$a^3+b^3=(a+b)^3-3ab(a+b)$</li>
<li>$x^2+\frac1{x^2}=\left(x+\frac1x\right)^2-2$, $\quad x^3+\frac1{x^3}=\left(x+\frac1x\right)^3-3\left(x+\frac1x\right)$</li>
<li>Sophie Germain: $a^4+4b^4=(a^2+2ab+2b^2)(a^2-2ab+2b^2)$</li>
<li>Simon's trick: $xy+ax+by=(x+b)(y+a)-ab$</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Uses on the AMC</h3>
<ul><li><b>Arithmetic shortcuts:</b> $2027^2-2026^2=4053$; $\;99\cdot101=100^2-1$.</li>
<li><b>Prime factors of $2^n-1$:</b> factor repeatedly, e.g. $2^{12}-1=(2^6-1)(2^6+1)=63\cdot65$.</li>
<li><b>Counting integer solutions:</b> $a^2-b^2=N$ means $(a-b)(a+b)=N$. Count factor pairs of the same parity.</li>
<li><b>Symmetric expressions:</b> rewrite $x^2+y^2$ or $x^3+y^3$ in terms of $x+y$ and $xy$.</li></ul>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example.</b> Find the largest prime factor of $5^4+4$.
<details><summary>Solution</summary>$5^4+4\cdot1^4=(25+10+2)(25-10+2)=37\cdot17$, so the answer is $37$.</details></div>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-alg-rad">
<h2>Radicals &amp; Absolute Value</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Radicals</h4><ul>
<li>Simplify by pulling out square factors: $\sqrt{72}=\sqrt{36\cdot2}=6\sqrt2$.</li>
<li>Rationalize with the conjugate: $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt a-\sqrt b}=\dfrac{\sqrt a+\sqrt b}{a-b}$.</li>
<li>Denesting: $\sqrt{a+2\sqrt b}=\sqrt x+\sqrt y$ where $x+y=a,\ xy=b$. Example: $\sqrt{7+4\sqrt3}=\sqrt{7+2\sqrt{12}}=2+\sqrt3$.</li>
<li>Infinite nesting: $x=\sqrt{c+\sqrt{c+\cdots}}$ satisfies $x^2=c+x$; take the positive root.</li>
</ul></div>
<div class="box"><h4>Absolute value</h4><ul>
<li>$|x-a|$ is the distance from $x$ to $a$ on the number line.</li>
<li>$|E|=k$ (with $k>0$) splits into $E=k$ or $E=-k$.</li>
<li>$|x-a|+|x-b|$ is at least $|a-b|$, with equality for every $x$ between $a$ and $b$. If it equals something larger, there are exactly two solutions, symmetric about $\frac{a+b}{2}$.</li>
<li>For mixed expressions, split into cases at the points where each expression inside is $0$.</li>
</ul></div>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example.</b> Solve $\sqrt{x+3-4\sqrt{x-1}}+\sqrt{x+8-6\sqrt{x-1}}=1$.
<details><summary>Solution</summary>With $t=\sqrt{x-1}$ the radicands are $(t-2)^2$ and $(t-3)^2$, so the equation is $|t-2|+|t-3|=1$, true for all $2\le t\le3$, i.e. every $x\in[5,10]$: infinitely many solutions.</details></div>
</script>
