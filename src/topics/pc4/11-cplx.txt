<h2>4.7 Complex Numbers</h2>
<p>The equation $x^2=-1$ has no real solution, because squares of real numbers are never negative. Mathematicians enlarged the number system by inventing a number $i$ with</p>
$$i^2=-1.$$
<p>A <b>complex number</b> is any number $a+bi$ with $a,b$ real. $a$ is the <b>real part</b> and $b$ the <b>imaginary part</b>. Real numbers are the complex numbers with $b=0$; numbers $bi$ with $b\ne0$ are <b>imaginary</b>. Two complex numbers are equal exactly when their real parts are equal <b>and</b> their imaginary parts are equal.</p>

<h3>1. Arithmetic</h3>
<p>Compute as with polynomials in $i$, then replace $i^2$ by $-1$.</p>
<ul><li><b>Add/subtract:</b> combine real and imaginary parts: $(3+2i)+(4-i)=7+i$.</li>
<li><b>Multiply:</b> FOIL: $(3+2i)(4-i)=12-3i+8i-2i^2=12+5i+2=14+5i$.</li></ul>
<div class="box"><h4>Powers of $i$ repeat every 4</h4>$i^1=i,\quad i^2=-1,\quad i^3=-i,\quad i^4=1$, then the pattern repeats. So $i^n$ depends only on the remainder when $n$ is divided by $4$: $i^{2023}=i^{3}=-i$ since $2023=4\cdot505+3$.</div>
<p>Any four consecutive powers add to $0$: $i+i^2+i^3+i^4=i-1-i+1=0$. So $i+i^2+\cdots+i^{100}=0$.</p>

<h3>2. Conjugates and division</h3>
<p>The <b>conjugate</b> of $z=a+bi$ is $\bar z=a-bi$. The key fact:</p>
$$(a+bi)(a-bi)=a^2+b^2,$$
<p>a nonnegative <b>real</b> number. To divide, multiply the numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator:</p>
$$\frac{2+3i}{1-i}=\frac{(2+3i)(1+i)}{(1-i)(1+i)}=\frac{2+2i+3i+3i^2}{1+1}=\frac{-1+5i}{2}=-\frac12+\frac52i.$$
<p>Useful special cases: $\frac1i=\frac{-i}{-i^2}=-i$, and $(1+i)^2=2i$, so $(1+i)^8=(2i)^4=16$.</p>

<h3>3. Square roots of negative numbers</h3>
<div class="box">For $b\gt0$: $\sqrt{-b}=\sqrt b\,i$. For example $\sqrt{-4}=2i$ and $\sqrt{-12}=2\sqrt3\,i$.</div>
<div class="warn"><h4>$\sqrt a\sqrt b=\sqrt{ab}$ fails for negatives</h4>$\sqrt{-4}\cdot\sqrt{-9}=(2i)(3i)=6i^2=-6$, <b>not</b> $\sqrt{36}=6$. Always convert $\sqrt{-b}$ to $\sqrt b\,i$ <i>before</i> multiplying.</div>

<h3>4. Complex solutions of quadratic equations</h3>
<p>With complex numbers, every quadratic equation has solutions. The quadratic formula works as usual; when $b^2-4ac\lt0$ the solutions are a <b>conjugate pair</b>.</p>
<p>$x^2+2x+5=0$: $x=\dfrac{-2\pm\sqrt{4-20}}{2}=\dfrac{-2\pm\sqrt{-16}}{2}=\dfrac{-2\pm4i}{2}=-1\pm2i$.</p>
<ul><li>$b^2-4ac\gt0$: two real solutions; $=0$: one real (double) solution; $\lt0$: two non-real conjugate solutions.</li></ul>

<h3>Worked examples</h3>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example 1.</b> Find real $a,b$ with $(a+bi)(1+2i)=-3+4i$.
<details><summary>Solution</summary>$a+bi=\frac{-3+4i}{1+2i}=\frac{(-3+4i)(1-2i)}{5}=\frac{-3+6i+4i+8}{5}=\frac{5+10i}{5}=1+2i$, so $a=1,b=2$.</details></div>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example 2.</b> Simplify $i^{14}+i^{15}$.
<details><summary>Solution</summary>$14=4\cdot3+2$ and $15=4\cdot3+3$, so $i^{14}+i^{15}=-1+(-i)=-1-i$.</details></div>
<div class="tip"><h4>Standard form</h4>Always finish with $a+bi$: no $i^2$, no $i$ in a denominator.</div>
