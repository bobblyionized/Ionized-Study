<h2>4.6 Polynomial and Rational Inequalities</h2>
<p>An inequality like $x^3\ge4x$ asks <b>for which $x$</b> one side is bigger. The answer is usually a union of intervals. The method in every case: get $0$ on one side, find where the other side is $0$ (or undefined), and check the sign in between.</p>

<h3>1. Basic principles</h3>
<div class="box"><ul>
<li>Adding or subtracting the same quantity on both sides keeps the inequality.</li>
<li>Multiplying or dividing both sides by a <b>positive</b> number keeps the direction.</li>
<li>Multiplying or dividing by a <b>negative</b> number <b>reverses</b> the direction.</li></ul></div>
<p><b>Linear:</b> $3-2x\le7\Rightarrow-2x\le4\Rightarrow x\ge-2$ (flipped when dividing by $-2$). <b>Compound:</b> $2\lt3x-1\le8\Rightarrow3\lt3x\le9\Rightarrow1\lt x\le3$, the interval $(1,3]$; do the same thing to all three parts.</p>

<h3>2. Polynomial inequalities</h3>
<ol><li>Rewrite as $f(x)\gt0$ (or $\lt,\ \ge,\ \le$) with $0$ on the right.</li>
<li>Find the roots of $f$ (factor). They split the number line into intervals.</li>
<li>On each interval, $f$ has one sign (polynomials are continuous, so they can only change sign at roots). Test one point per interval, or use the factor signs.</li>
<li>Collect the intervals with the right sign. For $\ge$ or $\le$, include the roots.</li></ol>
<p>Example: $x^3\ge4x\Rightarrow x^3-4x\ge0\Rightarrow x(x-2)(x+2)\ge0$. Roots $-2,0,2$. Testing $x=-3,-1,1,3$ gives signs $-,+,-,+$. Answer: $[-2,0]\cup[2,\infty)$.</p>
<div class="warn"><h4>Never divide by a variable</h4>Dividing $x^3\ge4x$ by $x$ to get $x^2\ge4$ is wrong: if $x\lt0$ the direction should flip, and you also lose the solution $x=0$. Move everything to one side and factor instead.</div>
<p><b>Quadratics:</b> $x^2-x-6\lt0\Rightarrow(x-3)(x+2)\lt0$. An upward parabola is negative between its roots: $(-2,3)$. If there are no real roots, the quadratic has one sign everywhere: $x^2+1\gt0$ for all $x$, and $x^2+1\lt0$ has no solutions.</p>
<p><b>Repeated roots:</b> the sign doesn't change at an even-multiplicity root. $(x-2)^2(x+1)\le0$: negative for $x\lt-1$, zero at $-1$ and $2$, positive elsewhere. Answer: $(-\infty,-1]\cup\{2\}$. Don't lose the isolated point!</p>

<h3>3. Rational inequalities</h3>
<p>The sign of $\frac{g(x)}{h(x)}$ can change only where $g=0$ or $h=0$. These are the <b>critical numbers</b>.</p>
<ol><li>Get $0$ on one side and combine into a single fraction. (<b>Don't</b> multiply by the denominator: its sign is unknown.)</li>
<li>Critical numbers: zeros of numerator and denominator.</li>
<li>Test each interval.</li>
<li>Zeros of the numerator are included for $\ge,\le$. Zeros of the denominator are <b>never</b> included.</li></ol>
<p>Example: $\dfrac{2x+1}{x-1}\le1\Rightarrow\dfrac{2x+1}{x-1}-1\le0\Rightarrow\dfrac{x+2}{x-1}\le0$. Critical numbers $-2,1$; the fraction is $\le0$ on $[-2,1)$.</p>
<p>Example: $\frac1x\lt2$. Multiplying by $x$ would be wrong for $x\lt0$. Instead $\frac1x-2\lt0\Rightarrow\frac{1-2x}{x}\lt0$. Critical numbers $0,\frac12$; solution $(-\infty,0)\cup\left(\frac12,\infty\right)$.</p>

<h3>Worked examples</h3>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example 1.</b> Solve $\dfrac{x-1}{x+3}\ge0$.
<details><summary>Solution</summary>Critical numbers $1$ (numerator) and $-3$ (denominator). Signs: $x\lt-3$: $\frac{-}{-}=+$; $-3\lt x\lt1$: $\frac{-}{+}=-$; $x\gt1$: $+$. Include $1$ (makes the fraction $0$), exclude $-3$. Answer: $(-\infty,-3)\cup[1,\infty)$.</details></div>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example 2.</b> How many integers satisfy $x^2\le10$?
<details><summary>Solution</summary>$-\sqrt{10}\le x\le\sqrt{10}$, about $-3.16\le x\le3.16$: the integers $-3,\dots,3$, which is $7$.</details></div>
<div class="tip"><h4>Sign-chart shortcut</h4>For a product of linear factors with positive leading coefficient, the sign is $+$ to the right of every root and flips each time you pass a root of odd multiplicity.</div>

Q: Solve $(x-2)^2(x+1)\le0$.
L: 2
A: (-\infty,-1]\cup\{2\}
W: (-\infty,-1]
W: [-1,\,2]
W: (-\infty,\,2]
W: [-1,\infty)
S: The roots are $-1$ (multiplicity $1$) and $2$ (multiplicity $2$).
$(x-2)^2\ge0$ always, so the sign of the product is the sign of $x+1$, except that the product is $0$ at $x=2$.
The product is $\le0$ when $x+1\le0$ (i.e. $x\le-1$) <b>or</b> when $x=2$.
Answer: $(-\infty,-1]\cup\{2\}$. The isolated point $x=2$ is easy to miss.

L: 2

L: 1
A: \text{all real numbers}
W: \text{no real numbers}
