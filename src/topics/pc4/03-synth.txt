<h2>4.2.A Synthetic Division</h2>
<p>Dividing by a linear factor $x-c$ comes up constantly (finding roots, testing factors, evaluating polynomials). <b>Synthetic division</b> is a shorthand for that long division that keeps only the coefficients.</p>

<h3>1. The procedure</h3>
<p>To divide $x^3-5x^2+3x+7$ by $x-2$:</p>
<ol><li>Write $c=2$ in the corner and the coefficients $1,\ -5,\ 3,\ 7$ in a row. <b>Include a $0$ for every missing power.</b></li>
<li>Bring the first coefficient down.</li>
<li>Multiply it by $c$, write the product under the next coefficient, and add. Repeat to the end.</li></ol>
<table class="t" style="width:auto"><tr><td>$2$</td><td>$1$</td><td>$-5$</td><td>$3$</td><td>$7$</td></tr>
<tr><td></td><td></td><td>$2$</td><td>$-6$</td><td>$-6$</td></tr>
<tr><td></td><td><b>$1$</b></td><td><b>$-3$</b></td><td><b>$-3$</b></td><td><b>$1$</b></td></tr></table>
<p>The bottom row gives the <b>quotient's coefficients</b> (one degree lower than the dividend) and, in the last position, the <b>remainder</b>:</p>
$$x^3-5x^2+3x+7=(x-2)(x^2-3x-3)+1.$$
<div class="warn"><h4>The sign of $c$</h4>For $x-c$ use $c$; for $x+3$ use $c=-3$. In synthetic division you <b>add</b> (long division subtracts, which is why the sign of $c$ flips).</div>

<h3>2. Why it works</h3>
<p>Each bottom-row number is what long division would produce at that step: bring down the leading coefficient, multiply by $c$, and combine with the next coefficient. Synthetic division is the same arithmetic as evaluating $f(c)$ by nesting: $x^3-5x^2+3x+7=((x-5)x+3)x+7$, and with $x=2$: $((2-5)2+3)2+7=1$.</p>

<h3>3. Uses</h3>
<ul><li><b>Evaluating:</b> the remainder is $f(c)$ (Remainder Theorem). For $2x^4+5x^3-x+7$ at $c=-3$: coefficients $2,5,0,-1,7$ give the bottom row $2,-1,3,-10,37$, so $f(-3)=37$.</li>
<li><b>Testing a root:</b> remainder $0$ means $c$ is a root and $x-c$ a factor.</li>
<li><b>Factoring:</b> after dividing out a root, the quotient (the <b>depressed polynomial</b>) has the remaining roots. $x^3-2x^2-9x+18$ divided by $x-3$ gives $x^2+x-6=(x+3)(x-2)$, so the roots are $3,-3,2$.</li></ul>

<h3>4. Dividing by $ax-b$</h3>
<p>Write $ax-b=a\left(x-\frac ba\right)$. Divide synthetically by $x-\frac ba$; the remainder is correct, and the quotient must then be <b>divided by $a$</b>. For $2x^3+3x^2-x+1$ divided by $2x-1$: use $c=\frac12$, getting $2,4,1$ with remainder $\frac32$. So dividing by $x-\frac12$ gives quotient $2x^2+4x+1$; dividing by $2x-1$ gives quotient $x^2+2x+\frac12$, remainder $\frac32$.</p>

<h3>Worked examples</h3>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example 1.</b> Divide $x^4-16$ by $x-2$.
<details><summary>Solution</summary>Coefficients $1,0,0,0,-16$ with $c=2$: bottom row $1,2,4,8,0$. Quotient $x^3+2x^2+4x+8$, remainder $0$, so $x-2$ is a factor.</details></div>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example 2.</b> Find the remainder when $2x^4-3x^2+x-4$ is divided by $x+1$.
<details><summary>Solution</summary>$c=-1$; coefficients $2,0,-3,1,-4$; bottom row $2,-2,-1,2,-6$. Remainder $-6$ (and indeed $f(-1)=2-3-1-4=-6$).</details></div>
<div class="tip"><h4>Speed tip</h4>On contests, synthetic division is the fastest way to evaluate a high-degree polynomial at an integer and to peel off roots one at a time.</div>

Q: When $2x^3+3x^2-x+1$ is divided by $2x-1$, what is the quotient?
L: 2
A: x^2+2x+\tfrac12
W: 2x^2+4x+1
W: x^2+2x+1
W: 2x^2+2x+\tfrac12
W: x^2+4x+1
S: $2x-1=2\left(x-\tfrac12\right)$. First divide by $x-\tfrac12$ with $c=\tfrac12$: coefficients $2,3,-1,1$.
Bring down $2$; $2\cdot\frac12=1\Rightarrow3+1=4$; $4\cdot\frac12=2\Rightarrow-1+2=1$; $1\cdot\frac12=\frac12\Rightarrow1+\frac12=\frac32$.
So $f(x)=\left(x-\tfrac12\right)(2x^2+4x+1)+\tfrac32=(2x-1)\left(x^2+2x+\tfrac12\right)+\tfrac32$.
Dividing by $2x-1$, the quotient is $x^2+2x+\frac12$ (the synthetic quotient divided by $2$) and the remainder is $\frac32$.
