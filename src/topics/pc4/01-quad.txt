<h2>4.1 Quadratic Functions</h2>
<p>A <b>quadratic function</b> has a rule of the form $f(x)=ax^2+bx+c$ with $a\ne0$. Its graph is a <b>parabola</b>, the same shape as $y=x^2$ but possibly stretched, flipped and moved. Every parabola has a turning point, the <b>vertex</b>, and is symmetric about the vertical line through it, the <b>axis of symmetry</b>.</p>

<h3>1. Vertex form</h3>
<div class="box">Every quadratic can be written in <b>vertex form</b>
$$f(x)=a(x-h)^2+k.$$
Its graph is $y=ax^2$ shifted so that the vertex is at $(h,k)$. If $a\gt0$ it opens <b>up</b> and $k$ is the minimum value; if $a\lt0$ it opens <b>down</b> and $k$ is the maximum value. $|a|\gt1$ makes it narrower, $|a|\lt1$ wider.</div>
<p>To get vertex form, <b>complete the square</b>: $2x^2+8x-1=2(x^2+4x)-1=2(x^2+4x+4)-8-1=2(x+2)^2-9$. Vertex $(-2,-9)$.</p>

<h3>2. The vertex formula</h3>
<div class="box">The vertex of $f(x)=ax^2+bx+c$ has $x$-coordinate $$h=-\frac{b}{2a},$$ and $y$-coordinate $k=f(h)$.</div>
<p>Why: the two $x$-intercepts (when they exist) are $\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}$, symmetric about their average $-\frac b{2a}$. More generally, any two points of the parabola at the same height are symmetric about the axis, so <b>the axis is halfway between them</b>. A parabola through $(-1,4)$ and $(5,4)$ has axis $x=2$.</p>
<div class="lfigs"><div class="lplot" data-f="x*x-6*x+5;-0.5*x*x-x+3" data-x="-6,7" data-y="-5,6" data-w="240" data-pts="3,-4;-1,3.5" data-cap="$x^2-6x+5$: vertex $(3,-4)$, opens up. $-\frac12x^2-x+3$: vertex $(-1,3.5)$, opens down"></div></div>

<h3>3. Reading a quadratic</h3>
<ul><li><b>$y$-intercept:</b> $f(0)=c$.</li>
<li><b>$x$-intercepts:</b> solve $ax^2+bx+c=0$. The discriminant $b^2-4ac$ tells how many: $2$ if positive, $1$ (vertex on the axis) if zero, none if negative.</li>
<li><b>Range:</b> $[k,\infty)$ if $a\gt0$, $(-\infty,k]$ if $a\lt0$.</li>
<li><b>Increasing/decreasing:</b> for $a\gt0$, decreasing on $(-\infty,h)$ and increasing on $(h,\infty)$; reversed for $a\lt0$.</li></ul>

<h3>4. Writing the rule from the vertex and one point</h3>
<p>Start from $f(x)=a(x-h)^2+k$ with the known vertex, then substitute the other point to find $a$. Vertex $(2,-3)$ through $(4,5)$: $5=a(4-2)^2-3$, so $4a=8$, $a=2$, and $f(x)=2(x-2)^2-3$.</p>

<h3>5. Applications: maximum and minimum problems</h3>
<p>Whenever a quantity is a quadratic function of a variable, its largest or smallest value is at the vertex.</p>
<ul><li><b>Fencing:</b> $120$ ft of fence for three sides of a rectangle along a river. With width $x$, length $120-2x$, the area $A=x(120-2x)=-2x^2+120x$ peaks at $x=-\frac{120}{2(-2)}=30$: area $30\cdot60=1800$ ft$^2$.</li>
<li><b>Projectiles:</b> $h(t)=-16t^2+v_0t+h_0$ reaches its maximum at $t=\frac{v_0}{32}$. For $h(t)=-16t^2+48t+6$: $t=1.5$, height $42$ ft.</li>
<li><b>Revenue:</b> if $x$ items sell when the price is $p=50-0.5x$, revenue $R=xp=50x-0.5x^2$ is maximized at $x=50$, $R=1250$.</li>
<li><b>Numbers:</b> two numbers with sum $24$ have product $x(24-x)$, maximized at $x=12$: $144$.</li></ul>

<h3>Worked examples</h3>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example 1.</b> Find the range of $f(x)=-3x^2+12x-7$.
<details><summary>Solution</summary>$h=-\frac{12}{2(-3)}=2$, $k=f(2)=-12+24-7=5$. It opens down, so the range is $(-\infty,5]$.</details></div>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example 2.</b> For which $c$ does the vertex of $y=x^2-10x+c$ lie on the $x$-axis?
<details><summary>Solution</summary>$h=5$, $k=25-50+c=c-25$. Setting $k=0$ gives $c=25$; then $y=(x-5)^2$.</details></div>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example 3.</b> A theater sells $300$ tickets at $\$8$. Each $\$1$ price increase loses $25$ customers. What price maximizes revenue?
<details><summary>Solution</summary>With $n$ one-dollar increases, $R=(8+n)(300-25n)=-25n^2+100n+2400$, maximized at $n=2$: price $\$10$, revenue $\$2500$.</details></div>
<div class="warn"><h4>Common traps</h4><ul>
<li>Sign error in the vertex formula: it's $-\frac{b}{2a}$. For $x^2-6x$, $h=+3$.</li>
<li>In vertex form, $(x-h)$ means the vertex is at $+h$: $(x+2)^2$ has $h=-2$.</li>
<li>Answering "where" (the $x$-value) when the question asks "what is the maximum" (the $y$-value), or vice versa.</li>
</ul></div>

Q: How many $x$-intercepts does the graph of $f(x)=x^2+4x+5$ have?
L: 1
A: 0
W: 1
W: 2
W: 3
W: 4
S: Complete the square: $x^2+4x+5=(x+2)^2+1$. The vertex is $(-2,1)$, above the $x$-axis, and the parabola opens up, so it never reaches the axis.
Equivalently, the discriminant is $4^2-4(1)(5)=-4\lt0$: no real roots.
