<script type="text/plain" id="L-poly-vieta">
<h2>Vieta's Formulas</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Cubic $ax^3+bx^2+cx+d$ with roots $r,s,t$</h4>
$r+s+t=-\dfrac ba,\qquad rs+st+tr=\dfrac ca,\qquad rst=-\dfrac da$<br>
The signs alternate $-,+,-,+,\dots$; the same pattern continues for quartics: $\sum r=-\frac ba$, $\sum rs=\frac ca$, $\sum rst=-\frac da$, $rstu=\frac ea$.</div>
<h3>Useful consequences</h3>
<ul><li>$r^2+s^2+t^2=(r+s+t)^2-2(rs+st+tr)$</li>
<li>$\frac1r+\frac1s+\frac1t=\frac{rs+st+tr}{rst}$</li>
<li>$(k-r)(k-s)(k-t)=\frac{p(k)}{a}$. For example $(1+r)(1+s)(1+t)=-\frac{p(-1)}{a}$ for a cubic.</li>
<li>If $1,2,3$ are roots of $x^3+ax^2+bx+c$, then $1+a+b+c=p(1)=0$.</li>
<li><b>Roots in arithmetic progression</b> $m-d, m, m+d$: the sum $3m$ gives the middle root. <b>Geometric progression</b> $\frac mq, m, mq$: the product $m^3$ gives the middle root.</li></ul>
<div class="tip"><h4>AMC tip</h4>If a root satisfies $r^3=r+1$, you can reduce high powers: $\sum r^3=\sum r+3$.</div>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-poly-rem">
<h2>Remainder &amp; Factor Theorems</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Theorems</h4><ul>
<li><b>Remainder theorem:</b> dividing $p(x)$ by $x-a$ leaves remainder $p(a)$.</li>
<li><b>Factor theorem:</b> $x-a$ divides $p(x)$ exactly when $p(a)=0$.</li>
<li>Dividing by a degree-$n$ divisor leaves a remainder of degree $\lt n$. For $(x-a)(x-b)$ the remainder is $mx+c$ with $ma+c=p(a)$ and $mb+c=p(b)$.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Common moves</h3>
<ul><li>Plug in the roots of the divisor: for $x^2-1$, use $x=\pm1$; for $x^3-x$, use $x=0,\pm1$.</li>
<li>Unknown coefficients: set $p(a)=0$ for each known factor and solve the system.</li>
<li><b>"$p(k)=k$" trick:</b> if a monic cubic has $p(1)=1, p(2)=2, p(3)=3$, then $p(x)-x=(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)$.</li></ul>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example.</b> Find the remainder of $x^{81}+x^{49}+x^{25}+x^9+x$ divided by $x^3-x$.
<details><summary>Solution</summary>The remainder $r(x)=ax^2+bx+c$ agrees with $p$ at $0,1,-1$: $r(0)=0$, $r(1)=5$, $r(-1)=-5$. So $c=0$, $a+b=5$, $a-b=-5$, giving $r(x)=5x$.</details></div>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-poly-roots">
<h2>Rational &amp; Integer Roots</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Rational root theorem</h4>
If $p(x)=a_nx^n+\dots+a_0$ has integer coefficients and $\frac pq$ (in lowest terms) is a root, then $p\mid a_0$ and $q\mid a_n$. For a monic polynomial, every rational root is an integer dividing $a_0$.</div>
<h3>Conjugate roots</h3>
<ul><li>Real coefficients: complex roots come in pairs $a\pm bi$, and $(x-(a+bi))(x-(a-bi))=x^2-2ax+a^2+b^2$.</li>
<li>Rational coefficients: $a+\sqrt b$ comes with $a-\sqrt b$, and the pair gives $x^2-2ax+a^2-b$.</li>
<li>$\sqrt2+\sqrt3$ has minimal polynomial $x^4-10x^2+1$: square, isolate the radical, square again.</li></ul>
<h3>Strategy</h3>
<p>Guess an easy root ($\pm1,\pm2$, or a factor of the constant over a factor of the leading coefficient), divide it out, and solve the remaining quadratic. Count distinct versus repeated roots carefully.</p>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-poly-sym">
<h2>Symmetric Sums &amp; Newton Sums</h2>
<p>Let $e_1=\sum r$, $e_2=\sum rs$, $e_3=\sum rst$, and $p_k=\sum r^k$ (the power sums).</p>
<div class="box"><h4>Newton's identities (first few)</h4>
$p_1=e_1,\quad p_2=e_1^2-2e_2,\quad p_3=e_1^3-3e_1e_2+3e_3$<br>
For a cubic $x^3+ax^2+bx+c$: $p_k=-ap_{k-1}-bp_{k-2}-cp_{k-3}$ for $k\ge4$. The same idea for a quadratic $x^2-sx+q$: $p_k=sp_{k-1}-qp_{k-2}$.</div>
<h3>Transforming roots</h3>
<table class="t"><tr><th>New roots</th><th>New polynomial</th></tr>
<tr><td>$r+k$</td><td>$p(x-k)$; the sum of roots increases by $nk$</td></tr>
<tr><td>$kr$</td><td>$p(x/k)$</td></tr>
<tr><td>$\frac1r$</td><td>reverse the coefficients</td></tr></table>
<h3>Sum of coefficients</h3>
<p>The sum of the coefficients of $p$ is $p(1)$. So the coefficients of $(3x-2)^{10}$ add up to $1^{10}=1$.</p>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-poly-coef">
<h2>Coefficients &amp; Polynomial Tricks</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Plug in special values</h4><ul>
<li>$p(1)$ is the sum of the coefficients; $p(-1)$ is the alternating sum.</li>
<li>Even-power coefficients sum to $\frac{p(1)+p(-1)}2$; odd-power coefficients to $\frac{p(1)-p(-1)}2$.</li>
<li>$p(0)$ is the constant term.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Degrees</h3>
<p>$\deg(pq)=\deg p+\deg q$ and $\deg p(q(x))=\deg p\cdot\deg q$. A degree-$n$ polynomial is determined by $n+1$ values.</p>
<h3>Coefficients of products</h3>
<p>In $(1+ax)(1+bx)(1+cx)$, the coefficient of $x^k$ is the sum of all products of $k$ of the numbers $a,b,c$ (the elementary symmetric sums).</p>
<h3>Construct a helper polynomial</h3>
<p>If $p(k)=\frac1k$ for $k=1,\dots,4$ and $\deg p=3$, then $xp(x)-1$ has roots $1,2,3,4$, so $xp(x)-1=c(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)(x-4)$. Plug in $x=0$ to find $c$.</p>
<h3>Difference equations</h3>
<p>If $p(x+1)-p(x)=2x+1$, then $p$ is a quadratic with leading coefficient $1$: here $p(x)=x^2+p(0)$.</p>
</script>
