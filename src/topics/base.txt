<script type="text/plain" id="L-base-conv">
<h2>Base Conversion &amp; Arithmetic</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Place value</h4>
$\overline{d_kd_{k-1}\cdots d_0}_b=d_kb^k+\dots+d_1b+d_0$, with every digit $0\le d_i\lt b$.</div>
<h3>Converting</h3>
<ul><li><b>Base $b$ to base 10:</b> expand the powers. $2143_5=2\cdot125+1\cdot25+4\cdot5+3=298$.</li>
<li><b>Base 10 to base $b$:</b> divide by $b$ repeatedly. The remainders, read from bottom to top, are the digits.</li>
<li>Base 2 to base 8 or 16: group the binary digits in threes or fours.</li>
<li>$n$ has $k$ digits in base $b$ exactly when $b^{k-1}\le n\lt b^k$.</li></ul>
<h3>Unknown bases</h3>
<p>Turn the equation into a polynomial in $b$: $121_b=(b+1)^2$, $34_b+25_b=61_b$ gives $5b+9=6b+1$. Remember that every digit must be less than $b$. That often decides between solutions.</p>
<h3>Arithmetic in base $b$</h3>
<p>Carry when a column reaches $b$. It is often easier to convert to base 10, compute, and convert back.</p>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-base-digit">
<h2>Digit Sums &amp; Place Value</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Tools</h4><ul>
<li>Two-digit $\overline{ab}=10a+b$. Reversal difference: $\overline{ab}-\overline{ba}=9(a-b)$. Three digits: $\overline{abc}-\overline{cba}=99(a-c)$.</li>
<li>A number is congruent to its digit sum mod 9.</li>
<li>Palindromes with $n$ digits: $9\cdot10^{\lceil n/2\rceil-1}$ (choose the first half; the first digit cannot be $0$).</li>
<li>Digits used to write $1,\dots,N$: count by length (1-digit numbers use 9 digits, 2-digit numbers use 180, ...).</li>
<li>Numbers with a given digit sum: use stars and bars, with the first digit at least $1$ and every digit at most $9$.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Smallest number with digit sum $S$</h3>
<p>Use as many 9s as possible at the end, and put the remainder in front: digit sum $30$ gives $3999$.</p>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-base-dec">
<h2>Terminating &amp; Repeating Decimals</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Facts</h4><ul>
<li>A reduced fraction $\frac ab$ terminates exactly when $b=2^m5^n$. It then has $\max(m,n)$ digits after the decimal point.</li>
<li>Otherwise it repeats. The period of $\frac1q$ (with $\gcd(q,10)=1$) is the smallest $k$ with $10^k\equiv1\pmod q$, which divides $\varphi(q)$.</li>
<li>Known periods: $\frac17$: 6 ($142857$), $\frac1{13}$: 6, $\frac1{11}$: 2, $\frac1{41}$: 5, $\frac1{27}$ and $\frac1{37}$: 3.</li>
<li>The $n$th digit after the decimal point: reduce $n$ modulo the period.</li>
</ul></div>
<p>For "how many $\frac n{D}$ terminate," the factors of $D$ other than 2 and 5 must cancel, so $n$ must be a multiple of that part of $D$.</p>
</script>
