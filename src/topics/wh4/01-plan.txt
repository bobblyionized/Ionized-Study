<h2>Unit 4: Chinese Dynasties</h2>
<p>This unit follows China from the chaos at the end of the Zhou Dynasty to the Ming, about 2,000 years. The same big ideas keep coming back: <b>strong central government</b>, <b>Confucian values</b>, the <b>civil service exam</b>, threats from <b>northern nomads</b> (and the Great Wall built against them), and a cycle of dynasties rising, prospering, and falling.</p>

<div class="cornell">
<div class="cn-top"><div><span class="cn-k">Topic</span>Unit 4: Chinese Dynasties, the big picture</div><div><span class="cn-k">Readings</span>pp. 137–143, 256–265, 310–329, 617–623</div>
<div class="cn-eq"><span class="cn-k">Essential question</span>Why did Chinese dynasties rise and fall, and what stayed the same through all of them?</div></div>
<div class="cn-h"><span>Cues</span><span>Notes</span></div>
<div class="cn-row"><div class="cn-cue">What problem started it all?</div><div class="cn-note"><ul><li>Late Zhou: lords acted like independent kings → constant war (<b>warring states period</b>).</li><li>Thinkers answered the chaos: <b>Confucianism</b> (morals, family), <b>Daoism</b> (nature, do little), <b>Legalism</b> (strict laws).</li></ul></div></div>
<div class="cn-row"><div class="cn-cue">Order of the dynasties</div><div class="cn-note"><ul><li><b>Zhou → Qin → Han</b> → (350 years of division) → <b>Sui → Tang → Song → Yuan → Ming</b> → Qing.</li><li>Memory hook: “<b>Q</b>uick <b>H</b>orses <b>S</b>print <b>T</b>oward <b>S</b>ome <b>Y</b>ellow <b>M</b>eadows”.</li></ul></div></div>
<div class="cn-row"><div class="cn-cue">Who unified China, and how?</div><div class="cn-note"><ul><li><b>Qin:</b> Shi Huangdi, by force and Legalism (221 BC).</li><li><b>Han:</b> Liu Bang, centralized government with lower taxes; Wudi expanded it.</li><li><b>Sui:</b> Wendi reunited north and south (589); Grand Canal.</li><li><b>Yuan:</b> Kublai Khan, a Mongol, conquered all of China (1279).</li><li><b>Ming:</b> Hongwu drove out the Mongols (1368).</li></ul></div></div>
<div class="cn-row"><div class="cn-cue">What stayed the same?</div><div class="cn-note"><ul><li><b>Confucian values</b> and the <b>civil service exam</b>: begun under the Han, expanded by the Tang and Song, restored by the Ming, lasted until 1912.</li><li>Fear of <b>northern nomads</b> → the Great Wall (Qin, Han, Ming).</li><li>The emperor as a semidivine link between heaven and earth.</li></ul></div></div>
<div class="cn-row"><div class="cn-cue">Why do dynasties fall?</div><div class="cn-note"><ul><li>Heavy taxes and forced labor (Qin, Sui, Tang, Yuan).</li><li>Weak rulers, palace plots, corruption (Qin, Han, Yuan, Ming).</li><li>Rich–poor gap, floods, famine → peasant revolts (Han, Yuan).</li><li>Outside invaders (Jurchen vs. Song, Mongols vs. Song, Manchus vs. Ming).</li></ul></div></div>
<div class="cn-row"><div class="cn-cue">Biggest achievements</div><div class="cn-note"><ul><li><b>Han:</b> paper (AD 105), collar harness, wheelbarrow, Silk Roads.</li><li><b>Tang/Song:</b> movable type, gunpowder, porcelain, compass, paper money, mechanical clock, fast-ripening rice.</li><li><b>Ming:</b> Forbidden City, Zheng He’s voyages, today’s Great Wall.</li></ul></div></div>
<div class="cn-sum"><span class="cn-k">Summary</span><p>China kept returning to strong, centralized rule held together by Confucian ideas and scholar-officials chosen by exam. Each dynasty rose by restoring order, grew rich through farming, trade, and inventions, and fell when taxes, corruption, revolts, or invaders became too much. Even foreign rulers (Mongols, Manchus) ended up ruling through Chinese traditions.</p></div>
