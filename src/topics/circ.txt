=== LESSONS ===
<script type="text/plain" id="L-circ-angle">
<h2>Inscribed &amp; Central Angles</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Angle–arc rules</h4><ul>
<li>A central angle equals its arc. An <b>inscribed angle is half</b> its intercepted arc.</li>
<li>Inscribed angles subtending the same arc are equal. An angle inscribed in a semicircle is $90^\circ$.</li>
<li>Two chords crossing <b>inside</b>: angle $=\frac12(\text{sum of the intercepted arcs})$.</li>
<li>Secants or tangents meeting <b>outside</b>: angle $=\frac12(\text{far arc}-\text{near arc})$.</li>
<li>Tangent–chord angle $=\frac12$ the intercepted arc.</li>
<li>Two tangents from $P$: $\angle P=180^\circ-(\text{minor arc})$.</li>
</ul></div>
<p>A regular $n$-gon's side subtends a central angle of $\frac{360}n$ and an inscribed angle (from the far arc) of $\frac{180}{n}$.</p>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-circ-tan">
<h2>Tangents &amp; Tangent Circles</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Facts</h4><ul>
<li>A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency.</li>
<li>Two tangent segments from an external point are equal. The length is $\sqrt{d^2-r^2}$, where $d$ is the distance to the center.</li>
<li>Tangent circles: the centers are $r_1+r_2$ apart (externally tangent) or $|r_1-r_2|$ apart (internally tangent).</li>
<li>Common external tangent length: $\sqrt{d^2-(r_1-r_2)^2}$. For externally tangent circles this is $2\sqrt{r_1r_2}$.</li>
<li>Common internal tangent length: $\sqrt{d^2-(r_1+r_2)^2}$.</li>
<li>A quadrilateral with an inscribed circle has $AB+CD=BC+DA$ (equal tangents).</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Circle tangent to both axes</h3>
<p>Its center is $(r,r)$. Plug in a point it passes through to get a quadratic in $r$.</p>
<h3>Circle in a quarter circle</h3>
<p>The center lies on the bisector, at distance $r\sqrt2$ from the corner, so $r\sqrt2+r=R$.</p>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-circ-pop">
<h2>Power of a Point &amp; Chords</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Power of a point</h4><ul>
<li>Chords $AB$ and $CD$ meeting at $E$ inside: $AE\cdot EB=CE\cdot ED$.</li>
<li>Secants from an outside point $P$: $PA\cdot PB=PC\cdot PD$ (near part × whole).</li>
<li>Tangent $PT$ and a secant: $PT^2=PA\cdot PB$.</li>
<li>All of these equal $|d^2-r^2|$, where $d=$ distance from $P$ to the center.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Chord geometry</h3>
<ul><li>A chord of length $c$ at distance $d$ from the center satisfies $\left(\frac c2\right)^2+d^2=r^2$.</li>
<li>The shortest chord through an interior point $P$ is perpendicular to $OP$, with length $2\sqrt{r^2-OP^2}$.</li>
<li><b>Sagitta:</b> for a chord $c$ whose arc rises $h$ above it, $r=\frac{c^2/4+h^2}{2h}$.</li></ul>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-circ-cyc">
<h2>Cyclic Quadrilaterals</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Properties of a cyclic quadrilateral $ABCD$</h4><ul>
<li>Opposite angles are supplementary: $A+C=B+D=180^\circ$. (The converse is true too.)</li>
<li>Angles subtending the same side are equal: $\angle BAC=\angle BDC$.</li>
<li><b>Ptolemy:</b> $AC\cdot BD=AB\cdot CD+BC\cdot DA$.</li>
<li><b>Brahmagupta:</b> area $=\sqrt{(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)(s-d)}$.</li>
<li>Rectangles and isosceles trapezoids are always cyclic. A rectangle's diagonal is a diameter.</li>
</ul></div>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example.</b> A regular pentagon has side 1. Find its diagonal $d$.
<details><summary>Solution</summary>Ptolemy on four vertices: $d\cdot d=d\cdot1+1\cdot1$, so $d^2-d-1=0$ and $d=\frac{1+\sqrt5}2$.</details></div>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-circ-arc">
<h2>Arcs, Sectors &amp; Circular Regions</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Formulas (angle $\theta$ in degrees)</h4><ul>
<li>Circumference $2\pi r$; area $\pi r^2$.</li>
<li>Arc length $=\frac{\theta}{360}\cdot2\pi r$; sector area $=\frac{\theta}{360}\cdot\pi r^2$.</li>
<li>Segment (between a chord and its arc) $=$ sector $-$ triangle $=\frac{\theta}{360}\pi r^2-\frac12r^2\sin\theta$.</li>
<li>Annulus $=\pi(R^2-r^2)$. If a chord of the big circle is tangent to the small one with length $2\ell$, the annulus has area $\pi\ell^2$.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Shaded regions</h3>
<p>Write the shaded area as (simple shape) $\pm$ (simple shape). Common pieces: a square inscribed in a circle (side $r\sqrt2$), a circle inscribed in a square ($r=\frac s2$), and the lens of two equal circles through each other's centers: $2\left(\frac{\pi r^2}{6}\cdot2-\frac{\sqrt3}4r^2\right)=\frac{2\pi}3r^2-\frac{\sqrt3}2r^2$.</p>
</script>
