<script type="text/plain" id="L-solid-prism">
<h2>Prisms, Cubes &amp; Cylinders</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Formulas</h4><ul>
<li>Box $a\times b\times c$: $V=abc$, surface area $2(ab+bc+ca)$, space diagonal $\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}$.</li>
<li>Cube of side $s$: $V=s^3$, surface area $6s^2$, space diagonal $s\sqrt3$.</li>
<li>Any prism: $V=(\text{base area})\times(\text{height})$.</li>
<li>Cylinder: $V=\pi r^2h$, lateral area $2\pi rh$, total $2\pi r^2+2\pi rh$.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Tricks</h3>
<ul><li><b>Face areas</b> $ab,bc,ca$ given: $V=\sqrt{(ab)(bc)(ca)}$.</li>
<li><b>Edge sum and diagonal</b> given: $(a+b+c)^2=(a^2+b^2+c^2)+2(ab+bc+ca)$, so surface area $=(a+b+c)^2-d^2$.</li>
<li><b>Painted $n\times n\times n$ cube:</b> 3 painted faces: 8 cubes; 2 faces: $12(n-2)$; 1 face: $6(n-2)^2$; none: $(n-2)^3$.</li></ul>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-solid-pyr">
<h2>Pyramids &amp; Cones</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Formulas</h4><ul>
<li>Pyramid or cone: $V=\frac13Bh$. Cone: $V=\frac13\pi r^2h$.</li>
<li>Cone slant height $\ell=\sqrt{r^2+h^2}$; lateral area $\pi r\ell$; total $\pi r^2+\pi r\ell$.</li>
<li>Square pyramid: height, half-base and slant height (to the midpoint of a base edge) form a right triangle.</li>
<li>Regular tetrahedron with edge $s$: $V=\dfrac{s^3\sqrt2}{12}$, height $s\sqrt{\frac23}$.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Frustums</h3>
<p>Cutting a cone or pyramid parallel to the base at a fraction $k$ of its height (from the apex) leaves a small similar solid with $k^3$ of the volume. The frustum has $1-k^3$.</p>
<h3>Cones from sectors</h3>
<p>Rolling a sector of radius $R$ and angle $\theta$ into a cone: the slant height is $R$, and the arc length becomes the base circumference: $\frac{\theta}{360}2\pi R=2\pi r$.</p>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-solid-sphere">
<h2>Spheres &amp; Inscribed Solids</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Formulas</h4><ul>
<li>Sphere: $V=\frac43\pi r^3$, surface area $4\pi r^2$.</li>
<li>Hemisphere (with its flat base): total area $3\pi r^2$.</li>
<li>Sphere inscribed in a cube of side $s$: $r=\frac s2$. Cube inscribed in a sphere: $r=\frac{s\sqrt3}{2}$. Sphere tangent to all 12 edges: $r=\frac{s\sqrt2}{2}$.</li>
<li>Archimedes: a sphere has $\frac23$ the volume (and the same lateral area) of its circumscribed cylinder.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Scaling and reshaping</h3>
<ul><li>Scaling lengths by $k$ scales areas by $k^2$ and volumes by $k^3$.</li>
<li>Melting and recasting keeps the volume: one sphere of radius $R$ makes $\left(\frac Rr\right)^3$ spheres of radius $r$.</li></ul>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-solid-dist">
<h2>3D Distances &amp; Cross-Sections</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Tools</h4><ul>
<li>Distance: $\sqrt{\Delta x^2+\Delta y^2+\Delta z^2}$.</li>
<li><b>Shortest path on a box's surface:</b> unfold the faces into a plane. Between opposite corners of an $a\times b\times c$ box with $c$ largest, it is $\sqrt{(a+b)^2+c^2}$. Check all three unfoldings when unsure.</li>
<li>Cube center to vertex: $\frac{s\sqrt3}2$; center to a face center: $\frac s2$; between centers of adjacent faces: $\frac{s\sqrt2}{2}$.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Cube cross-sections</h3>
<ul><li>A plane through the three neighbors of a vertex gives an equilateral triangle of side $s\sqrt2$.</li>
<li>A plane through the midpoints of six edges (perpendicular to a space diagonal) gives a regular hexagon of side $\frac{s\sqrt2}{2}$.</li>
<li>A plane through two opposite edges gives a rectangle $s\times s\sqrt2$.</li></ul>
<h3>Scale models</h3>
<p>With scale $1:k$, volumes scale by $k^3$ and surface areas by $k^2$.</p>
</script>

Q: A rectangular box has faces with areas $12$, $15$, and $20$. What is its volume?
L: 2
A: 60
W: 47
W: 120
W: 240
W: 30
S: Let the edges be $a,b,c$, so the face areas are $ab=12$, $bc=15$, $ca=20$.
Multiply all three: $(ab)(bc)(ca)=a^2b^2c^2=(abc)^2=12\cdot15\cdot20=3600$.
Volume $abc=\sqrt{3600}=60$. (The edges are $3,4,5$: e.g. $ab=12$, $bc=15$ give $\frac ac=\frac45$, and with $ca=20$, $a=4$, $c=5$, $b=3$.)

Q: A rectangular box has edges summing to $48$ (all $12$ edges) and a space diagonal of $9$. What is its surface area?
L: 3
A: 63
W: 72
W: 81
W: 144
W: 54

The space diagonal gives $a^2+b^2+c^2=81$.
Surface area is $2(ab+bc+ca)$, and $(a+b+c)^2=a^2+b^2+c^2+2(ab+bc+ca)$.
So $2(ab+bc+ca)=144-81=63$.

$V=(2\sqrt3)^3=8\cdot3\sqrt3=24\sqrt3$.

$V=\pi r^2h=\pi\cdot9\cdot4=36\pi$.

Base area $6^2=36$, so $V=\frac13\cdot36\cdot4=48$.

Q: What is the volume of a regular tetrahedron with edge length $6$?
L: 2
A: 18\sqrt{2}
W: 36\sqrt{2}
W: 54
W: 9\sqrt{2}
W: 18\sqrt{3}
S: A regular tetrahedron with edge $s$ has volume $\frac{s^3\sqrt2}{12}$.
(Derivation: base area $\frac{\sqrt3}4s^2$, height $s\sqrt{\frac23}$, and $\frac13\cdot\frac{\sqrt3}{4}s^2\cdot s\sqrt{\frac23}=\frac{s^3\sqrt2}{12}$.)
$\frac{216\sqrt2}{12}=18\sqrt2$.

Q: A $240^\circ$ sector of a circle with radius $6$ is rolled into a cone. What is the cone's volume?
L: 3
A: \frac{32\sqrt{5}\pi}{3}
W: 16\sqrt{5}\pi
W: \frac{64\pi}{3}
W: 32\pi
W: \frac{16\sqrt{5}\pi}{3}
S: When the sector rolls up, its radius becomes the slant height ($\ell=6$) and its arc becomes the base circumference.
Arc length: $\frac{240}{360}\cdot2\pi\cdot6=8\pi=2\pi r$, so $r=4$.
Height: $h=\sqrt{\ell^2-r^2}=\sqrt{36-16}=2\sqrt5$.
$V=\frac13\pi r^2h=\frac13\pi\cdot16\cdot2\sqrt5=\frac{32\sqrt5\pi}{3}$.
---
Q: A cone of volume $64\pi$ is cut by a plane parallel to its base, halfway up its height. What is the volume of the bottom piece (the frustum)?
L: 2
A: 56\pi
W: 32\pi
W: 48\pi
W: 8\pi
W: 60\pi
S: The piece above the cut is a smaller cone, similar to the original with ratio $\frac12$ (half the height, half the radius).
Volumes scale by the cube of the ratio: $\left(\frac12\right)^3=\frac18$, so the top cone is $\frac18\cdot64\pi=8\pi$.
The frustum is the rest: $64\pi-8\pi=56\pi$.

Q: A square pyramid has base side $10$, and its slant height (from apex to the midpoint of a base edge) is $13$. What is its volume?

W: 520
S: The slant height, the height, and half the base side form a right triangle (apex, center of the base, midpoint of an edge).
$h=\sqrt{13^2-5^2}=\sqrt{144}=12$.
$V=\frac13\cdot10^2\cdot12=400$.

Q: What is the ratio of a sphere's volume to the volume of the smallest cylinder containing it?

Q: An ant walks on the surface of a unit cube from one vertex to the opposite vertex. What is the shortest possible path length?
L: 2
A: \sqrt{5}
W: \sqrt{3}
W: 2
W: 1+\sqrt{2}
W: 3
S: Unfold the cube so the ant's path lies on two adjacent faces laid flat: they form a $1\times2$ rectangle, with the start and end at opposite corners.
The shortest path is the straight diagonal: $\sqrt{1^2+2^2}=\sqrt5$.
(The straight line through the cube, $\sqrt3$, isn't allowed: the ant stays on the surface.)

Q: A plane through the midpoints of six edges of a unit cube cuts a regular hexagon. What is the hexagon's area?
L: 3
A: \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4}
W: \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}
W: 1
W: \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{2}
W: \sqrt{2}
S: The six edge midpoints that this plane passes through form a regular hexagon. Two adjacent ones differ by $\frac12$ in two coordinates, so the side is $\sqrt{\frac14+\frac14}=\frac{\sqrt2}{2}$.
A regular hexagon with side $s$ has area $\frac{3\sqrt3}{2}s^2=\frac{3\sqrt3}{2}\cdot\frac12=\frac{3\sqrt3}{4}$.
---
Q: A plane passes through the three vertices of a unit cube that are adjacent to one vertex. What is the area of the cross-section?

S: The three vertices adjacent to a corner are pairwise joined by face diagonals of length $\sqrt2$, so the cross-section is an equilateral triangle with side $\sqrt2$.
Area $=\frac{\sqrt3}{4}(\sqrt2)^2=\frac{\sqrt3}{4}\cdot2=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}$.

Q: An ant travels on the surface of a $2\times3\times4$ box from one corner to the opposite corner. What is the shortest possible path length?
L: 3
A: \sqrt{41}
W: \sqrt{29}
W: \sqrt{45}
W: 9
