<script type="text/plain" id="L-solid-prism">
<h2>Prisms, Cubes &amp; Cylinders</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Formulas</h4><ul>
<li>Box $a\times b\times c$: $V=abc$, surface area $2(ab+bc+ca)$, space diagonal $\sqrt{a^2+b^2+c^2}$.</li>
<li>Cube of side $s$: $V=s^3$, surface area $6s^2$, space diagonal $s\sqrt3$.</li>
<li>Any prism: $V=(\text{base area})\times(\text{height})$.</li>
<li>Cylinder: $V=\pi r^2h$, lateral area $2\pi rh$, total $2\pi r^2+2\pi rh$.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Tricks</h3>
<ul><li><b>Face areas</b> $ab,bc,ca$ given: $V=\sqrt{(ab)(bc)(ca)}$.</li>
<li><b>Edge sum and diagonal</b> given: $(a+b+c)^2=(a^2+b^2+c^2)+2(ab+bc+ca)$, so surface area $=(a+b+c)^2-d^2$.</li>
<li><b>Painted $n\times n\times n$ cube:</b> 3 painted faces: 8 cubes; 2 faces: $12(n-2)$; 1 face: $6(n-2)^2$; none: $(n-2)^3$.</li></ul>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-solid-pyr">
<h2>Pyramids &amp; Cones</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Formulas</h4><ul>
<li>Pyramid or cone: $V=\frac13Bh$. Cone: $V=\frac13\pi r^2h$.</li>
<li>Cone slant height $\ell=\sqrt{r^2+h^2}$; lateral area $\pi r\ell$; total $\pi r^2+\pi r\ell$.</li>
<li>Square pyramid: height, half-base and slant height (to the midpoint of a base edge) form a right triangle.</li>
<li>Regular tetrahedron with edge $s$: $V=\dfrac{s^3\sqrt2}{12}$, height $s\sqrt{\frac23}$.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Frustums</h3>
<p>Cutting a cone or pyramid parallel to the base at a fraction $k$ of its height (from the apex) leaves a small similar solid with $k^3$ of the volume. The frustum has $1-k^3$.</p>
<h3>Cones from sectors</h3>
<p>Rolling a sector of radius $R$ and angle $\theta$ into a cone: the slant height is $R$, and the arc length becomes the base circumference: $\frac{\theta}{360}2\pi R=2\pi r$.</p>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-solid-sphere">
<h2>Spheres &amp; Inscribed Solids</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Formulas</h4><ul>
<li>Sphere: $V=\frac43\pi r^3$, surface area $4\pi r^2$.</li>
<li>Hemisphere (with its flat base): total area $3\pi r^2$.</li>
<li>Sphere inscribed in a cube of side $s$: $r=\frac s2$. Cube inscribed in a sphere: $r=\frac{s\sqrt3}{2}$. Sphere tangent to all 12 edges: $r=\frac{s\sqrt2}{2}$.</li>
<li>Archimedes: a sphere has $\frac23$ the volume (and the same lateral area) of its circumscribed cylinder.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Scaling and reshaping</h3>
<ul><li>Scaling lengths by $k$ scales areas by $k^2$ and volumes by $k^3$.</li>
<li>Melting and recasting keeps the volume: one sphere of radius $R$ makes $\left(\frac Rr\right)^3$ spheres of radius $r$.</li></ul>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-solid-dist">
<h2>3D Distances &amp; Cross-Sections</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Tools</h4><ul>
<li>Distance: $\sqrt{\Delta x^2+\Delta y^2+\Delta z^2}$.</li>
<li><b>Shortest path on a box's surface:</b> unfold the faces into a plane. Between opposite corners of an $a\times b\times c$ box with $c$ largest, it is $\sqrt{(a+b)^2+c^2}$. Check all three unfoldings when unsure.</li>
<li>Cube center to vertex: $\frac{s\sqrt3}2$; center to a face center: $\frac s2$; between centers of adjacent faces: $\frac{s\sqrt2}{2}$.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Cube cross-sections</h3>
<ul><li>A plane through the three neighbors of a vertex gives an equilateral triangle of side $s\sqrt2$.</li>
<li>A plane through the midpoints of six edges (perpendicular to a space diagonal) gives a regular hexagon of side $\frac{s\sqrt2}{2}$.</li>
<li>A plane through two opposite edges gives a rectangle $s\times s\sqrt2$.</li></ul>
<h3>Scale models</h3>
<p>With scale $1:k$, volumes scale by $k^3$ and surface areas by $k^2$.</p>
</script>
