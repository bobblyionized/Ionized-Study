<script type="text/plain" id="L-prob-basic">
<h2>Classical Probability</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Basics</h4><ul>
<li>For equally likely outcomes: $P=\dfrac{\#\text{favorable}}{\#\text{total}}$.</li>
<li>Complement: $P(\text{at least one})=1-P(\text{none})$.</li>
<li>Without replacement: multiply conditional fractions $\left(\frac4{52}\cdot\frac3{51}\right)$ or use combinations $\left(\binom42\big/\binom{52}2\right)$.</li>
<li>Two dice: $36$ outcomes; the number of ways to roll a sum $s$ is $6-|s-7|$.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Watch out</h3>
<ul><li>Make sure the outcomes you count are equally likely. The sums of two dice are <i>not</i>.</li>
<li>"Product is even" is the complement of "both odd": $1-\frac14=\frac34$.</li></ul>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-prob-cond">
<h2>Conditional Probability &amp; Independence</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Definitions</h4><ul>
<li>$P(A\mid B)=\dfrac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}$: restrict to the outcomes where $B$ happened.</li>
<li>Independent: $P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B)$. Then $P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A)P(B)$.</li>
<li><b>Bayes:</b> $P(A\mid B)=\dfrac{P(B\mid A)P(A)}{P(B)}$. Organize these with a tree or a table of counts.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Classics</h3>
<ul><li>Two children, at least one boy: $P(\text{two boys})=\frac13$ (the outcomes BB, BG, GB remain).</li>
<li>A rare disease plus a decent test still gives a low $P(\text{disease}\mid+)$: false positives from the large healthy group dominate.</li>
<li>Choose an urn at random, then draw: $P(\text{urn A}\mid\text{red})$ is proportional to $P(\text{red}\mid\text{A})$.</li></ul>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-prob-geo">
<h2>Geometric Probability</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Idea</h4>
When a point is chosen uniformly, the probability is the ratio of the favorable length, area, or volume to the total.</div>
<h3>Standard setups</h3>
<ul><li>Two numbers $x,y\in[0,1]$: draw the unit square and shade the region where the condition holds. For example, $x+y\lt 1$ is half the square.</li>
<li><b>Meeting problem:</b> two people arrive uniformly in a window of length $T$ and each waits $w$. $P(\text{meet})=1-\left(1-\frac wT\right)^2$.</li>
<li>Dartboards: the ratio of areas, so the square of the ratio of radii.</li>
<li>Three random points on a circle form a triangle containing the center with probability $\frac14$.</li>
<li>A stick broken at two random points forms a triangle with probability $\frac14$.</li></ul>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-prob-ev">
<h2>Expected Value</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Tools</h4><ul>
<li>$E[X]=\sum x\,P(X=x)$.</li>
<li><b>Linearity:</b> $E[X+Y]=E[X]+E[Y]$, <i>even when $X$ and $Y$ are dependent</i>.</li>
<li><b>Indicators:</b> to count something, add up the probabilities of each little event. For example, expected fixed points of a random permutation $=n\cdot\frac1n=1$.</li>
<li>A fair $n$-sided die has $E=\frac{n+1}2$. $n$ flips with heads probability $p$ give $np$ heads on average.</li>
<li>Waiting for a success with probability $p$: $E=\frac1p$ trials.</li>
</ul></div>
<p>Expected number of distinct faces in $k$ rolls: $6\left(1-\left(\frac56\right)^k\right)$, adding the indicator "face $i$ appears" over all faces.</p>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-prob-states">
<h2>States &amp; Recursion</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Method</h4>
Define a variable for each state (the probability of winning from there, or the expected remaining steps). Write one equation per state from what can happen next, then solve the system.</div>
<h3>Results worth knowing</h3>
<ul><li><b>Gambler's ruin</b> (fair, start at $i$, stop at $0$ or $N$): $P(\text{reach }N)=\frac iN$, and the expected number of steps is $i(N-i)$.</li>
<li>Expected flips until HH: $6$; until HT: $4$.</li>
<li>$k$ players take turns, each succeeding with probability $p$ (with $q=1-p$): the first player wins with probability $\frac{p}{1-q^k}$.</li>
<li>Best-of-3 with win probability $p$: $p^2+2p^2(1-p)$.</li></ul>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example.</b> Expected flips to get HH.
<details><summary>Solution</summary>Let $E$ be the expectation from the start and $E_H$ after a head. $E=1+\frac12E_H+\frac12E$ and $E_H=1+\frac12\cdot0+\frac12E$. Solving gives $E=6$.</details></div>
</script>

Q: Two fair dice are rolled. What is the probability that the sum is $7$?
L: 1
A: \frac{1}{6}
W: \frac{1}{12}
W: \frac{7}{36}
W: \frac{5}{36}
W: \frac{1}{36}
S: There are $6\cdot6=36$ equally likely ordered outcomes.
Sum $7$: $(1,6),(2,5),(3,4),(4,3),(5,2),(6,1)$, which is $6$ outcomes.
Probability $\frac{6}{36}=\frac16$. ($7$ is the most likely sum.)

Q: Three fair coins are flipped. What is the probability of at least two heads?
L: 1
A: \frac{1}{2}
W: \frac{3}{8}
W: \frac{1}{4}
W: \frac{3}{4}
W: \frac{5}{8}
S: There are $2^3=8$ equally likely outcomes.
Exactly $2$ heads: $\binom32=3$ outcomes (HHT, HTH, THH). Exactly $3$: $1$ (HHH).
$\frac{3+1}{8}=\frac12$. (By symmetry, "at least 2 heads" and "at least 2 tails" are equally likely and can't both happen.)

Q: Two cards are drawn from a standard deck without replacement. What is the probability both are aces?
L: 2
A: \frac{1}{221}
W: \frac{1}{169}
W: \frac{1}{26}
W: \frac{2}{221}
W: \frac{1}{13}
S: Multiply step by step: the first card is an ace with probability $\frac4{52}$; given that, the second is an ace with probability $\frac3{51}$ (one ace and one card are gone).
$\frac4{52}\cdot\frac3{51}=\frac{12}{2652}=\frac1{221}$.
(Or count: $\frac{\binom42}{\binom{52}2}=\frac{6}{1326}=\frac1{221}$.)

W: \frac{2}{5}

Favorable: $33+20-6=47$. Probability $\frac{47}{100}$.

$\frac{6+15}{45}=\frac{21}{45}=\frac7{15}$.

Q: A three-digit number is chosen at random. What is the probability that its digits are strictly increasing from left to right?
L: 3
A: \frac{7}{75}
W: \frac{1}{10}
W: \frac{21}{250}
W: \frac{1}{6}
W: \frac{2}{25}
S: A three-digit number has $900$ possibilities ($100$–$999$).
Strictly increasing digits can't include $0$: it would have to be the first digit. So pick any $3$ distinct digits from $1$–$9$; there's exactly one increasing order: $\binom93=84$.
$\frac{84}{900}=\frac{7}{75}$.

Q: A disease affects $1\%$ of people. A test is positive for $90\%$ of sick people and for $10\%$ of healthy people. Given a positive test, what is the probability the person is sick?
L: 3
A: \frac{1}{12}
W: \frac{9}{10}
W: \frac{1}{10}
W: \frac{1}{11}
W: \frac{1}{9}

(The disease is so rare that false positives vastly outnumber true ones.)

Q: Urn A has $3$ red and $1$ blue ball; urn B has $1$ red and $3$ blue. An urn is chosen at random and a red ball is drawn. What is the probability it came from urn A?

A: \frac{3}{4}

W: \frac{2}{3}
W: \frac{1}{4}

$P(\text{red})=\frac12\cdot\frac34+\frac12\cdot\frac14=\frac38+\frac18=\frac12$.
$P(A\mid\text{red})=\dfrac{3/8}{1/2}=\dfrac34$.

Q: Numbers $x$ and $y$ are chosen independently and uniformly from $[0,1]$. What is the probability that $x+y\lt 1$?
L: 1
A: \frac{1}{2}
W: \frac{1}{4}
W: \frac{1}{3}
W: \frac{2}{3}
W: \frac{3}{4}

$x+y\lt1$ is the triangle below the diagonal from $(1,0)$ to $(0,1)$, with area $\frac12$.

Q: Two friends arrive at independent random times between 1:00 and 2:00. Each waits $15$ minutes for the other. What is the probability they meet?
L: 2
A: \frac{7}{16}
W: \frac{1}{4}
W: \frac{9}{16}
W: \frac{1}{2}
W: \frac{3}{8}
S: Measure time in hours after 1:00: $(x,y)$ is uniform in the unit square. They meet when $|x-y|\le\frac14$.
They miss in two corner triangles ($y\gt x+\frac14$ and $x\gt y+\frac14$), each a right triangle with legs $\frac34$: total area $2\cdot\frac12\cdot\left(\frac34\right)^2=\frac{9}{16}$.
Meet: $1-\frac{9}{16}=\frac{7}{16}$.

Q: Numbers $x$ and $y$ are chosen uniformly from $[0,2]$. What is the probability that $xy\lt 1$?
L: 3
A: \frac{1+2\ln 2}{4}
W: \frac{1}{2}
W: \frac{\ln 2}{2}
W: \frac{1+\ln 2}{4}
W: \frac{1}{4}
S: $(x,y)$ is uniform in the $2\times2$ square (area $4$); find the area where $xy\lt1$.
For $0\le x\le\frac12$, every $y\le2$ works ($xy\le1$): area $\frac12\cdot2=1$.
For $\frac12\lt x\le2$, we need $y\lt\frac1x$: area $\int_{1/2}^{2}\frac{dx}{x}=\ln2-\ln\frac12=2\ln2$.
$P=\frac{1+2\ln2}{4}$.

Q: Three points are chosen at random on a circle. What is the probability that the triangle they form contains the center?
L: 2
A: \frac{1}{4}
W: \frac{1}{2}
W: \frac{1}{3}
W: \frac{1}{8}
W: \frac{3}{4}
S: The triangle misses the center exactly when all three points lie in some semicircle.
For each point, the other two land in the semicircle starting at it (going clockwise) with probability $\frac14$; these three events are disjoint, so $P(\text{same semicircle})=3\cdot\frac14=\frac34$.
Contains the center: $1-\frac34=\frac14$.
---
Q: A stick is broken at two uniformly random points. What is the probability the three pieces can form a triangle?
L: 2
A: \frac{1}{4}
W: \frac{1}{2}
W: \frac{1}{3}
W: \frac{3}{4}
W: \frac{1}{8}
S: Let the breaks be at $x$ and $y$ in a stick of length $1$: $(x,y)$ is uniform in the unit square. The pieces form a triangle exactly when each piece is less than $\frac12$ (triangle inequality).
For $x\lt y$ that means $x\lt\frac12$, $y\gt\frac12$, $y-x\lt\frac12$: a triangle of area $\frac18$. By symmetry the case $y\lt x$ adds another $\frac18$.
Probability $\frac14$.

Q: A fair coin is flipped until two heads appear in a row. What is the expected number of flips?
L: 2
A: 6
W: 4
W: 3
W: 8
W: 5
S: Use states: $E$ = expected flips from scratch, $E_H$ = expected flips when the last flip was H.
From scratch: one flip, then H (go to state H) or T (start over): $E=1+\frac12E_H+\frac12E$.
From H: one flip, then H (done) or T (start over): $E_H=1+\frac12\cdot0+\frac12E$.
Substitute: $E=1+\frac12\left(1+\frac12E\right)+\frac12E=\frac32+\frac34E$, so $\frac14E=\frac32$ and $E=6$.
---
Q: A fair coin is flipped until the pattern HT (heads then tails) appears. What is the expected number of flips?

W: 3
W: 8
W: 5

Wait for the first H: expected $2$ flips. After that, any H keeps you in the "last flip was H" state, and the first T finishes: expected $2$ more.
Total: $4$.

Q: A frog starts at $2$ on the number line and jumps $+1$ or $-1$ with equal probability, stopping when it reaches $0$ or $4$. What is the expected number of jumps?
L: 2
A: 4
W: 2
W: 3
W: 6
W: 8
S: Let $E_i$ be the expected jumps from $i$, with $E_0=E_4=0$ and $E_i=1+\frac12(E_{i-1}+E_{i+1})$.
The solution is $E_i=i(N-i)$ with $N=4$: from $2$, $E=2\cdot2=4$.
(Check: $E_1=3$, $E_2=4$, $E_3=3$; indeed $4=1+\frac12(3+3)$.)

Q: A team wins each game independently with probability $\frac23$. What is the probability it wins a best-of-three series?
L: 2
A: \frac{20}{27}
W: \frac{2}{3}

Q: A fair die is rolled until a $6$ appears. What is the probability that the number of rolls is even?
L: 3
A: \frac{5}{11}
W: \frac{1}{2}
W: \frac{6}{11}
W: \frac{5}{6}
