<script type="text/plain" id="L-prob-basic">
<h2>Classical Probability</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Basics</h4><ul>
<li>For equally likely outcomes: $P=\dfrac{\#\text{favorable}}{\#\text{total}}$.</li>
<li>Complement: $P(\text{at least one})=1-P(\text{none})$.</li>
<li>Without replacement: multiply conditional fractions $\left(\frac4{52}\cdot\frac3{51}\right)$ or use combinations $\left(\binom42\big/\binom{52}2\right)$.</li>
<li>Two dice: $36$ outcomes; the number of ways to roll a sum $s$ is $6-|s-7|$.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Watch out</h3>
<ul><li>Make sure the outcomes you count are equally likely. The sums of two dice are <i>not</i>.</li>
<li>"Product is even" is the complement of "both odd": $1-\frac14=\frac34$.</li></ul>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-prob-cond">
<h2>Conditional Probability &amp; Independence</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Definitions</h4><ul>
<li>$P(A\mid B)=\dfrac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}$: restrict to the outcomes where $B$ happened.</li>
<li>Independent: $P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B)$. Then $P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A)P(B)$.</li>
<li><b>Bayes:</b> $P(A\mid B)=\dfrac{P(B\mid A)P(A)}{P(B)}$. Organize these with a tree or a table of counts.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Classics</h3>
<ul><li>Two children, at least one boy: $P(\text{two boys})=\frac13$ (the outcomes BB, BG, GB remain).</li>
<li>A rare disease plus a decent test still gives a low $P(\text{disease}\mid+)$: false positives from the large healthy group dominate.</li>
<li>Choose an urn at random, then draw: $P(\text{urn A}\mid\text{red})$ is proportional to $P(\text{red}\mid\text{A})$.</li></ul>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-prob-geo">
<h2>Geometric Probability</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Idea</h4>
When a point is chosen uniformly, the probability is the ratio of the favorable length, area, or volume to the total.</div>
<h3>Standard setups</h3>
<ul><li>Two numbers $x,y\in[0,1]$: draw the unit square and shade the region where the condition holds. For example, $x+y\lt 1$ is half the square.</li>
<li><b>Meeting problem:</b> two people arrive uniformly in a window of length $T$ and each waits $w$. $P(\text{meet})=1-\left(1-\frac wT\right)^2$.</li>
<li>Dartboards: the ratio of areas, so the square of the ratio of radii.</li>
<li>Three random points on a circle form a triangle containing the center with probability $\frac14$.</li>
<li>A stick broken at two random points forms a triangle with probability $\frac14$.</li></ul>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-prob-ev">
<h2>Expected Value</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Tools</h4><ul>
<li>$E[X]=\sum x\,P(X=x)$.</li>
<li><b>Linearity:</b> $E[X+Y]=E[X]+E[Y]$, <i>even when $X$ and $Y$ are dependent</i>.</li>
<li><b>Indicators:</b> to count something, add up the probabilities of each little event. For example, expected fixed points of a random permutation $=n\cdot\frac1n=1$.</li>
<li>A fair $n$-sided die has $E=\frac{n+1}2$. $n$ flips with heads probability $p$ give $np$ heads on average.</li>
<li>Waiting for a success with probability $p$: $E=\frac1p$ trials.</li>
</ul></div>
<p>Expected number of distinct faces in $k$ rolls: $6\left(1-\left(\frac56\right)^k\right)$, adding the indicator "face $i$ appears" over all faces.</p>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-prob-states">
<h2>States &amp; Recursion</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Method</h4>
Define a variable for each state (the probability of winning from there, or the expected remaining steps). Write one equation per state from what can happen next, then solve the system.</div>
<h3>Results worth knowing</h3>
<ul><li><b>Gambler's ruin</b> (fair, start at $i$, stop at $0$ or $N$): $P(\text{reach }N)=\frac iN$, and the expected number of steps is $i(N-i)$.</li>
<li>Expected flips until HH: $6$; until HT: $4$.</li>
<li>$k$ players take turns, each succeeding with probability $p$ (with $q=1-p$): the first player wins with probability $\frac{p}{1-q^k}$.</li>
<li>Best-of-3 with win probability $p$: $p^2+2p^2(1-p)$.</li></ul>
<div class="ex"><b class="lbl">Example.</b> Expected flips to get HH.
<details><summary>Solution</summary>Let $E$ be the expectation from the start and $E_H$ after a head. $E=1+\frac12E_H+\frac12E$ and $E_H=1+\frac12\cdot0+\frac12E$. Solving gives $E=6$.</details></div>
</script>
