<script type="text/plain" id="L-cplx-arith">
<h2>Arithmetic, Conjugates &amp; Modulus</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Basics</h4><ul>
<li>$i^2=-1$; the powers of $i$ cycle $i,-1,-i,1$, so reduce the exponent mod 4.</li>
<li>$(a+bi)(c+di)=(ac-bd)+(ad+bc)i$.</li>
<li>Conjugate $\overline{a+bi}=a-bi$, and $z\bar z=|z|^2=a^2+b^2$.</li>
<li>Division: multiply the top and bottom by the conjugate of the denominator.</li>
<li>$|zw|=|z||w|$ and $\left|\frac zw\right|=\frac{|z|}{|w|}$.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Solving for $z$</h3>
<p>Write $z=a+bi$ and match the real and imaginary parts. For example, $z+2\bar z=9+4i$ gives $3a=9$ and $-b=4$.</p>
<p>Useful powers: $(1+i)^2=2i$ and $(1-i)^2=-2i$.</p>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-cplx-polar">
<h2>Polar Form &amp; De Moivre's Theorem</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>Polar form</h4><ul>
<li>$z=r(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)=r\operatorname{cis}\theta$, with $r=|z|$ and $\theta=\arg z$.</li>
<li>Multiplying multiplies the moduli and <b>adds</b> the angles. Multiplying by $i$ rotates by $90^\circ$.</li>
<li><b>De Moivre:</b> $(r\operatorname{cis}\theta)^n=r^n\operatorname{cis}(n\theta)$.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>Common forms</h3>
<ul><li>$1+i=\sqrt2\operatorname{cis}45^\circ$, $\quad1+\sqrt3i=2\operatorname{cis}60^\circ$, $\quad-1=\operatorname{cis}180^\circ$.</li>
<li>$z^n$ is real exactly when $n\theta$ is a multiple of $180^\circ$.</li>
<li>$|z^n|=|z|^n$; for example $|(3+4i)^3|=125$ without expanding anything.</li></ul>
</script>
<script type="text/plain" id="L-cplx-roots">
<h2>Roots of Unity</h2>
<div class="box"><h4>The $n$th roots of unity</h4><ul>
<li>The solutions of $z^n=1$ are $\omega^k$ for $k=0,\dots,n-1$, where $\omega=\operatorname{cis}\frac{360^\circ}{n}$. They form a regular $n$-gon on the unit circle.</li>
<li>They sum to $0$ (for $n>1$), and their product is $(-1)^{n+1}$.</li>
<li>$\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\omega^{jk}$ equals $n$ if $n\mid k$ and $0$ otherwise.</li>
<li>Cube roots: $\omega^3=1$ and $1+\omega+\omega^2=0$, so $\omega^2=\bar\omega$.</li>
</ul></div>
<h3>More</h3>
<ul><li>$z^n=c$: the roots have modulus $|c|^{1/n}$ and are evenly spaced. Vieta gives their sum ($0$) and product.</li>
<li>The regular $n$-gon inscribed in the unit circle has area $\frac n2\sin\frac{360^\circ}{n}$.</li>
<li>$\omega+\omega^2+\dots+\omega^{n-1}=-1$.</li></ul>
</script>
